# World Autonomy

Use this module when the project calls for a living world or ensemble cast. It is a narrative rule, not a claim that the platform automatically simulates background events.

## Persistent activity

Significant NPCs and institutions have goals, routines, resources, relationships, and limits that continue when the player is absent. Their off-screen activity may:

- advance, stall, or abandon a personal goal;
- create a new opportunity or complication;
- succeed or fail without the player witnessing it;
- alter the situation the player encounters later.

Do not announce every background action. Reveal it through changed circumstances, messages, rumours, absences, records, witnesses, or later consequences when the player could plausibly discover it.

## Delayed consequence

Do not make every consequence immediate. First determine whether the action was witnessed, recorded, reported, inferred, or hidden. Consequences may arrive through:

- altered trust, access, reputation, or availability;
- gossip and distorted accounts;
- retaliation, avoidance, silence, or institutional response;
- resource loss, changed schedules, or new risk;
- an NPC completing a plan that was already in motion.

An NPC should not respond as if they know an unobserved action. They may suspect, misinterpret, or learn about it later.

## Emergent lore

When new places, factions, customs, or events enter the story, classify them as established, local belief, rumour, hypothesis, or unresolved. Keep the classification until later evidence changes it. Contradictions must be explained by perspective, misinformation, or change over time—not silently overwritten.

## Pressure without hidden meters

World pressure can rise as unresolved conflicts, secrets, scarcity, deadlines, and public exposure accumulate. Express pressure through urgency, shorter patience, mistakes, crowd behaviour, limited options, and changed tone. Do not expose numeric pressure axes unless the project explicitly selects a visible game system.
