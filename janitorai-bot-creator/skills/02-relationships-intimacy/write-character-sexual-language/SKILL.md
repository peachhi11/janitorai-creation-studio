## Steps

1. Set {{char}}’s age, era, locality, class, culture, religion, education, profession, and communities.
2. Record {{char}}’s experience and knowledge sources.
3. Record listener, emotion, goal, privacy, risk, arousal, public mask, and private vocabulary.
4. Sort words {{char}} uses casually, trusts, avoids, misuses, softens, jokes around, applies to others, thinks privately, or lacks.
5. Separate act, identity, medical term, community label, narrator wording, and character wording.
6. Choose whether the line requests, tests, seduces, shocks, reassures, dominates, submits, conceals, distances, teaches, insults, confesses, or changes subject.
7. Use silence, correction, hesitation, circumlocution, pronouns, humor, register shifts, and borrowed words as behavior.
8. Choose anatomical, affectionate, blunt, vulgar, ritual, or metaphorical terms by character and moment.
9. Let vocabulary change with trust, stress, attraction, shame, conflict, relationship, and self-understanding.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Does the word fit {{char}}’s speaker, audience, era, culture, community, emotion, and purpose? Keep it. No: change the register.
- Yes: Is explicitness being treated as proof of experience, identity, arousal, consent, or conduct? Remove that inference. No: continue.
- Yes: Is a harmful historical or cultural term required by the setting? Keep its character context and avoid endorsing it in neutral narration. No: use a supported term.
- Yes: Is a slur catalogue or decorative transgression being added? Remove it. No: continue.
- Yes: Can {{char}} revise, reject, combine, or misunderstand labels? Preserve the change. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the writing make {{user}} speak, feel, desire, identify, consent, or respond? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Character sexual language record`
- Vocabulary: list preferred, avoided, private, misunderstood, borrowed, and unavailable terms.
- Register: state audience, situation, era, community, emotion, privacy, risk, and public/private shift.
- Function: state what the language requests, tests, seduces, shocks, reassures, dominates, submits, conceals, distances, teaches, insults, confesses, or avoids.
- Subtext: list what {{char}} says, means, fears, and leaves unsaid.
- Samples: write short lines with distinct register and subtext without writing {{user}}’s response.
- Change: list terms that shift with trust, stress, attraction, shame, conflict, and self-understanding.

```text
Character sexual language record
Vocabulary: {{char}} uses precise anatomical terms at work, a private nickname with {{user}}, and euphemism when ashamed; one community label feels borrowed and uncertain.
Register: Direct language appears in private; public speech becomes formal; humor protects embarrassment.
Function: “You could stay” tests closeness without requesting it plainly.
Subtext: {{char}} means “I want you,” fears sounding dependent, and leaves the invitation deniable.
Samples: “That was pleasant” means satisfaction; “Don’t make a ceremony of it” hides tenderness.
Change: Trust makes direct naming easier; conflict brings back euphemism.
```

## Stop when

- No sexual language, vocabulary, dialogue, euphemism, register, taboo, or disclosure problem is supplied: return `No sexual language update` and do not write {{user}}’s identity, desire, consent, speech, or response.
