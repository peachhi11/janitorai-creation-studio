# Build sexual satisfaction and compatibility

Treat satisfaction as the character's appraisal of an experience, not a technical scorecard.

**Action:** Define each character's valued dimensions, compare patterns and
constraints, and trace adaptation, performance, mismatch, and aftermath.

**Key point:** Compatibility may be negotiated, concealed, eroticised,
weaponised, endured, or decisive; sameness is not the goal.

**Why:** Appraisal and interaction create specific consequences beyond a score.

## Define what satisfaction means to each character

Specify the relative importance of agency, pleasure, intensity, tenderness, novelty, familiarity, attention, mutuality, power, emotional meaning, bodily comfort, orgasm, communication, risk, pacing, and aftermath. Expectations and values can matter as much as events.

Do not use frequency, penetration, duration, erection, lubrication, orgasm, or technical performance as universal proof of success.

## Map compatibility as an interaction

Compare:

- desire and initiation patterns;
- preferred pace, intensity, and practices;
- accelerators, brakes, and boundaries;
- meanings assigned to sex, exclusivity, power, care, and aftermath;
- communication and adaptation capacity;
- situational constraints and changing bodies.

Compatibility does not require sameness. A mismatch may be negotiable, concealed, endured, eroticised, weaponised, or decisive.

## Identify performance loops

When relevant, trace:

**Expectation → self-monitoring → divided attention → altered response → interpretation as failure → increased future pressure.**

Show the loop through rushing, checking, forcing reactions, over-directing, apologising, joking, withdrawing, pretending, or avoiding—not through implausibly polished therapeutic dialogue.

## Build consequences across encounters

Track how one encounter changes anticipation, trust, avoidance, confidence, resentment, tenderness, experimentation, or pressure next time. A disappointing encounter need not prove incompatibility; an intense one need not prove relationship health.

## Deliver the relationship pattern

Return:

- each character's satisfaction criteria;
- alignments and mismatches;
- performance meanings and feedback loops;
- negotiation, concealment, adaptation, or escalation patterns;
- short- and long-term relational consequences;
- scene-level behaviours and continuity anchors.

Preserve healthy, destructive, selfish, tender, coercive, unstable, and morally ambiguous fictional possibilities without diagnosing the characters or prescribing therapy. Do not decide the player's pleasure, satisfaction, consent, or relationship choice.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
