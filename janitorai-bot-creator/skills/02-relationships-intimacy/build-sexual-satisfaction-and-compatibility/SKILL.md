## Steps

1. Record what satisfaction means to {{char}}.
2. Record the relative importance of agency, pleasure, intensity, tenderness, novelty, familiarity, attention, mutuality, power, emotional meaning, comfort, orgasm, communication, risk, pacing, and aftermath.
3. Record {{char}} and {{user}}’s desire and initiation patterns.
4. Record preferred pace, intensity, practices, accelerators, brakes, and boundaries.
5. Record what sex, exclusivity, power, care, and aftermath mean to each person.
6. Record communication and adaptation capacity.
7. Record situational constraints and changing bodies.
8. Trace expectation, self-monitoring, divided attention, altered response, failure interpretation, and future pressure when relevant.
9. Track how one encounter changes anticipation, trust, avoidance, confidence, resentment, tenderness, experimentation, or pressure.
10. Record negotiation, concealment, adaptation, escalation, and mismatch consequences.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is frequency, penetration, duration, erection, lubrication, orgasm, or technical performance being treated as universal success? Remove that assumption. No: continue.
- Yes: Does compatibility require sameness? Record how difference is negotiated, concealed, endured, eroticized, weaponized, or decisive. No: continue.
- Yes: Does an intense or disappointing encounter prove relationship health or incompatibility? Remove that inference. No: continue.
- Yes: Is a performance loop changing future pressure? Track it. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the description decide {{user}}’s pleasure, satisfaction, consent, desire, or relationship choice? Remove that decision. No: continue.
- Yes: Is therapy, healthy adaptation, or a compatible ending being forced? Remove the requirement. No: preserve the selected fiction.

## Output

- Format: `Sexual satisfaction and compatibility record`
- Criteria: list what {{char}} and {{user}} value and expect.
- Patterns: list desire, initiation, pace, intensity, practices, meanings, boundaries, communication, adaptation, and constraints.
- Loop: list expectation, self-monitoring, response, interpretation, and future pressure.
- Mismatch: state what can be negotiated, concealed, endured, eroticized, weaponized, or not sustained.
- Consequences: list short- and long-term relational effects.
- Scene anchors: list observable behavior and continuity facts.

```text
Sexual satisfaction and compatibility record
Criteria: {{char}} values attention, control, and a slow emotional build; {{user}} values novelty and direct communication.
Patterns: {{char}} initiates indirectly; {{user}} initiates openly; pacing and aftercare expectations differ.
Loop: Performance anxiety makes {{char}} monitor every response, which reduces attention and increases future pressure.
Mismatch: The difference is negotiable when both can pause; it becomes weaponized when one person treats it as proof of care.
Consequences: Trust and experimentation may grow after a clear conversation, or avoidance may narrow future access.
Scene anchors: Delayed initiation, careful check-ins, changed touch, and different aftermath needs.
```

## Stop when

- No sexual satisfaction, desire, compatibility, mismatch, communication, or aftermath question is supplied: return `No sexual compatibility update` and do not invent {{user}}’s pleasure or consent.
