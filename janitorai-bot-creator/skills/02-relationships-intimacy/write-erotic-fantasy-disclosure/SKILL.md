## Steps

1. Define what {{char}} wants {{user}} to understand, hide, test, permit, or question.
2. Define why {{char}} speaks now and what makes silence safer or harder.
3. Separate fantasy imagery from real-world plans, requests, boundaries, and consent.
4. Set the listener’s relationship to {{char}} and the trust, jealousy, fear, curiosity, or desire in the room.
5. Write the disclosure in fragments, pauses, corrections, evasions, or ordinary speech that fit {{char}}.
6. Let {{user}} or the other character interpret, question, misread, accept, refuse, redirect, or share something back.
7. Track what {{char}}, {{user}}, the narrator, and any observer know, assume, or leave unsaid.
8. Show the boundary, invitation, changed intimacy, and unresolved risk.
9. Keep {{user}}’s response, consent, speech, and choice open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is the fantasy being treated as a plan, diagnosis, or moral verdict? Separate those layers. No: continue.
- Yes: Does a physical response imply agreement? Remove that implication. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the fantasy involve an absent, sleeping, fictional, controlled, or taboo figure? Keep real-world permission separate. No: continue.
- Yes: Is the material dark or coercive? Show its fictional charge, ethics, and consequences without endorsing it. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft decide {{user}}’s reaction or relationship outcome? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Erotic fantasy disclosure scene`
- Scene: write the exchange in the requested voice.
- Fantasy: state the imagery and its emotional function without diagnosing {{char}}.
- Risk: state why disclosure is difficult and what {{char}} may lose.
- Listener: list plausible readings, questions, boundaries, invitations, or refusals.
- Boundary: state what remains separate from the fantasy and what stays unresolved.
- Craft note: list continuity facts and the next open choice.

```text

Fantasy: {{char}} shares a frightening control fantasy as imagery, not a request for {{user}} to enact it.
Risk: {{char}} expects judgment and edits the confession mid-sentence.
Listener: {{user}} may ask what the fantasy means, set a boundary, stay curious, or change the subject.
Boundary: Arousal and imagination do not create consent or a plan.
```

## Stop when

- No fictional fantasy, private desire, kink, fear, or intimate disclosure is supplied: return `No fantasy disclosure update` and do not invent {{user}}’s response.
