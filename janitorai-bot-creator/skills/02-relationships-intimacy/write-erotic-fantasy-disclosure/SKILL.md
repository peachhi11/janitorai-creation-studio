# Write erotic fantasy disclosure

Treat a fantasy as information about imagination, mood, power, memory, fear, or curiosity—not a confession of intent and not a demand for enactment.

**Action:** Map what the speaker wants understood, what makes disclosure risky,
and which boundary remains separate from the imagery.

**Key point:** A fantasy can be dark, private, or arousing without becoming a
request, plan, diagnosis, or moral verdict.

**Why:** Keeping imagination, action, and response distinct preserves intimacy
without flattening the character's erotic logic.

## Build the disclosure

Choose:

- what the speaker wants the listener to understand, hide, test, or permit;
- why they speak now and what makes silence harder or safer;
- the difference between the fantasy’s imagery and the speaker’s real-world boundaries;
- the listener’s relationship to the speaker, including trust, surprise, jealousy, fear, or desire;
- what each person knows, assumes, and leaves unsaid.

Use sensory setting, pauses, corrections, evasions, and ordinary speech. Let the fantasy arrive in fragments if that fits the character. Keep the character’s private contradiction visible without translating it into a diagnosis.

## Keep fantasy and action separate

The listener may be curious, aroused, unsettled, amused, quiet, sceptical, or unwilling. They may ask questions, set a boundary, offer a modified version, change the subject, or disclose a fantasy of their own. A physical response is not agreement. A fantasy involving an absent, sleeping, fictional, controlled, or taboo figure is not permission to act on a real person.

## Write the turn

Shape the exchange as:

**safe opening → partial disclosure → listener’s reading → clarification or misreading → boundary or invitation → changed intimacy**

If the fantasy is dark, preserve its imaginative charge while making the ethics and consequences legible through character action. Do not make the narrator endorse it merely because the prose is sensual.

## Deliverable

Draft the scene in the requested voice, then provide a private craft note: fantasy content, emotional function, speaker’s risk, listener’s options, explicit boundary, and what remains unresolved.

Do not force enactment, reassurance, disclosure, or a healthy relationship
outcome. Leave the player's response open when the player owns it.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
