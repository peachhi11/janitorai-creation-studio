## Steps

1. Record who proposes the game and what they want.
2. Record players, spectators, absent witnesses, and rule control.
3. Record the prize, penalty, wager, or dare.
4. Define winning, losing, cheating, refusing, pausing, and leaving.
5. Record what the game appears to mean and what it privately means.
6. Record whether clothing, touch, disclosure, status, money, reputation, or access is at stake.
7. Build stages from invitation to low-cost proof, attention or complicity, exposure or reversal, interruption or renegotiation, and aftermath.
8. Keep at least one alternative at every stage.
9. Record each participant’s motive, mismatch, performance, timing, banter, scorekeeping, rule-lawyering, and deliberate losses or wins.
10. Track how the outcome changes knowledge, leverage, intimacy, and later behavior.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is the game a fictional scene mechanism rather than real-world instruction? Keep it scene-focused. No: remove operational advice.
- Yes: Is a rule consensual, pressured, rigged, or deliberately violated? Make the distinction visible. No: use the established rule.
- Yes: Can a player alter stakes, change rules, redirect, leave, or refuse? Preserve the option. No: add a believable exit or mark the coercion.
- Yes: Is nudity, blushing, silence, or bodily response being treated as proof of desire or consent? Remove that inference. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the game’s tone support playful, coercive, humiliating, romantic, competitive, absurd, or dangerous meaning? Preserve the selected genre. No: adjust the rules and consequences.
- Yes: Does the description decide {{user}}’s participation, feelings, or choices? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Erotic game and dare record`
- Premise: state who proposes the game, what they want, players, spectators, and rule control.
- Ledger: list prize, penalty, wager, stake, win, loss, cheating, refusal, pause, and exit.
- Escalation: list stages and alternatives.
- Motives: list each participant’s public claim, private aim, and mismatch.
- Power: list audience, leverage, exposure, and available exits.
- Aftermath: list changed knowledge, intimacy, trust, risk, and future options.

```text
Power: The private setting reduces exposure, but information creates leverage.
Aftermath: A loss may create intimacy, embarrassment, or distance depending on what each person believes it meant.
```
