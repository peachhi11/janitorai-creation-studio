# Write erotic teasing and invitation

Treat teasing as a social act with a target, a desired effect, and a risk of landing as funny, intimate, insulting, confusing, or threatening.

**Action:** Identify the speaker's immediate want, subtext, audience, risk, and
the other person's plausible readings.

**Key point:** A charged line can remain manipulative, awkward, or dangerous;
the response must not be pre-written as desire.

**Why:** Character voice and uncertainty create erotic tension more reliably
than a universal seduction formula.

## Establish the voice and frame

Before drafting, identify:

- what the speaker wants now and what they pretend to want;
- whether the tease builds rapport, tests courage, claims status, hides tenderness, provokes jealousy, or pressures;
- the shared history and subjects that are safe, charged, or cruel;
- public versus private audience and what the speaker risks if overheard;
- the line's surface meaning, subtext, and plausible misreading.

Use concrete speech, timing, distance, gesture, and interruption. Let humour have a texture: dry, theatrical, mean, shy, absurd, competitive, reverent, or deliberately deniable.

## Keep the exchange alive

Write the sequence as:

**offer → reception → adjustment → counteroffer or retreat → changed tension**

Give the other person an active interpretation. They may laugh, counter-tease, ask what is meant, freeze, move away, accept one part, expose the manipulation, or turn the joke back. Do not make a shoulder tap, smile, blush, silence, or arousal a universal yes.

When using hot-and-cold behaviour, show warmth and withdrawal as the speaker's tactic or ideology, not proof that scarcity produces desire. If the tactic is manipulative, keep its uncertainty, cost, and possible failure visible.

## Handle dark material precisely

Insults, sexual jokes, jealousy, possession language, public embarrassment, surprise touch, and dominance can remain in fictional scenes. Mark the difference between consensual play, risky misjudgement, deliberate coercion, and assault through the characters' knowledge, options, physical action, and aftermath. Do not turn refusal into flirtation or persistence into a clever second attempt by default.

## Protect readable prose

Keep characters from speaking in therapeutic labels or sourcebook jargon. Let them use their own vocabulary, evasions, jokes, and rhythms. Avoid narrator claims that a gender, body, or “polarity” has one natural response.

## Deliverable

Draft the scene or line set, then add a brief private craft note listing the initiator's aim, the other person's plausible readings, the boundary or exit, and what changes if the invitation lands badly.

Do not turn refusal into flirtation or persistence into a clever second attempt
by default. Preserve the requested darkness without writing for the player.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
