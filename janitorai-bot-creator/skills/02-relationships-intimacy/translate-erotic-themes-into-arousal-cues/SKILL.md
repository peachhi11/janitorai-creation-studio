# Translate erotic themes into arousal cues

Turn an author-facing erotic pattern into attention, tension, and behaviour that belongs to the character.

**Action:** Infer only from repeated evidence, map cue to meaning and conflict,
then translate it into attention, concealment, choice, and risk.

**Key point:** A theme may heighten, inhibit, redirect, or fail to matter; it is
not a universal aphrodisiac.

**Why:** Meaning-first cues create individual erotic tension.

## Identify the provisional theme

Infer a recurring theme only from repeated evidence across fantasies, attractions, memories, or scenes. Phrase it as an experience the character seeks, not a diagnostic label. Permit several themes, contradictions, and contexts where the pattern does not apply.

## Map meaning to cues

For each cue, specify:

- what the character perceives;
- the meaning they assign it;
- the emotion or conflict it activates;
- whether it heightens, inhibits, or redirects attention;
- what they do, conceal, imagine, delay, refuse, or risk next.

Longing, uncertainty, secrecy, prohibition, power, surrender, exposure, recognition, anger, guilt, fear, tenderness, pride, and shame are possible meanings—not universal aphrodisiacs.

## Use obstacles carefully

Distance, delay, rivalry, difference, risk, and ambivalence may intensify desire for a particular character. They may also extinguish it or make action unacceptable. Never turn another person's refusal into an obstacle to overcome. A boundary can be salient in fantasy and absolute in reality.

## Write embodied expression

Show the theme through gaze, attention, timing, withheld movement, rehearsal, selection, jokes, silence, changing distance, loss or recovery of composure, fantasy fragments, and later choices. Keep analytic terms out of dialogue unless they fit the speaker.

## Deliver the translation

Return:

- provisional theme and supporting evidence;
- cue-to-meaning map;
- accelerators, inhibitors, and reversals;
- observable public and private behaviours;
- variations across partners and contexts;
- scene openings that preserve consent and player choice.

Do not treat ambivalence as consent, distress as secretly wanted, emotional intensity as safety, or fantasy salience as permission. Do not assign the player's arousal, desire, fantasy, interpretation, speech, or action.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
