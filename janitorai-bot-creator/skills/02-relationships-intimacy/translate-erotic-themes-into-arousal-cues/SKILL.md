## Steps

1. Infer a provisional theme from repeated fantasies, attractions, memories, or scenes.
2. Phrase the theme as an experience {{char}} seeks rather than a diagnosis.
3. Record what {{char}} perceives.
4. Record what {{char}} thinks the cue means.
5. Record emotion, conflict, and whether attention heightens, inhibits, or redirects.
6. Record what {{char}} does, conceals, imagines, delays, refuses, or risks next.
7. Test distance, delay, rivalry, difference, risk, and ambivalence as possible accelerators, inhibitors, or reversals.
8. Translate the theme through gaze, attention, timing, withheld movement, rehearsal, selection, jokes, silence, distance, composure, fantasy fragments, and later choices.
9. Vary the pattern across partners and contexts.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is the theme supported by repeated evidence? Treat it as provisional. No: do not infer a recurring theme.
- Yes: Can the cue heighten, inhibit, redirect, or fail to matter? Preserve the variation. No: use the supported effect.
- Yes: Is refusal being treated as an obstacle to overcome? Preserve the boundary. No: continue.
- Yes: Is ambivalence being treated as consent, distress as secretly wanted, or intensity as safety? Remove that inference. No: continue.
- Yes: Is fantasy salience being treated as permission or conduct? Separate imagination and action. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the description decide {{user}}’s arousal, desire, fantasy, interpretation, speech, or action? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Erotic theme and arousal cue record`
- Theme: state the provisional experience and supporting evidence.
- Cue map: list perception, meaning, emotion, conflict, attention effect, behavior, concealment, and risk.
- Variation: list accelerators, inhibitors, reversals, partner differences, and context differences.
- Scene behavior: list public and private expressions.
- Openings: list scene openings that preserve consent and player choice.

```text
Erotic theme and arousal cue record
Theme: {{char}} seeks recognition without having to ask for it; repeated evidence comes from fantasy, selective attention, and indirect invitations.
Cue map: {{user}} remembers a private detail → {{char}} reads it as being seen → attention sharpens and shame competes → {{char}} jokes, delays, and risks a small disclosure.
Variation: Privacy heightens the cue; public attention inhibits it; a refusal ends the approach rather than creating a challenge.
Scene behavior: Publicly composed, privately attentive to timing and small signs of recognition.
Openings: A private joke, a direct but optional question, or a pause that leaves {{user}} room to choose.
```

## Stop when

- No erotic theme, cue, tension, fantasy, attraction, or behavior problem is supplied: return `No erotic arousal-cue update` and do not invent {{user}}’s desire or arousal.
