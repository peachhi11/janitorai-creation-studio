# Write digital courtship and texting

Do NOT use this skill to impose response-time games, compulsory disclosure, or a healthy relationship script.

Treat the medium as part of the relationship, not as transparent dialogue.

**Action:** Use platform affordances, timing, message texture, risk, and silence to reveal what each character wants and will not say.
**Key point:** Digital intimacy can be playful, manipulative, erotic, evasive, or unfinished; do not force a clean relationship script.
**Why:** The medium changes courtship and conflict without taking agency away from the people composing the messages.

## Define the channel

Establish platform, privacy, audience, permanence, editing, notification habits, message limits, identity certainty, and whether screenshots or forwarding matter. Account for language, disability, work, caregiving, access, safety, and time zones.

Clarify how each character normally uses the channel: rapid fragments, composed paragraphs, voice notes, images, reactions, formal punctuation, delayed batches, or practical messages only.

## Set the exchange's job

Choose the immediate purpose:

- reopen contact or reference a shared moment;
- maintain an existing rhythm;
- learn one specific thing;
- flirt within already supported context;
- clarify interest or repair a misread;
- propose a concrete plan;
- negotiate privacy, boundaries, or sexual tone;
- end, pause, or redefine contact.

Do not make every message perform attraction, status, and logistics at once.

## Build message turns

For each turn, track:

**Message → intended tone → plausible readings → reply, silence, or channel change → revised expectation.**

Use callbacks, specificity, incomplete thoughts, edits, emoji, punctuation, and timing according to voice. Let ambiguity arise from the medium without making confusion mandatory.

Response time is not a precise interest meter. Double texts, quick replies, silence, and read receipts acquire meaning from pattern and context.

## Move between channels

When appropriate, convert chat into a concrete invitation with enough detail to accept, alter, or decline. Show what changes when characters move from app to private number, text to call, public thread to direct message, or message to meeting.

Do not treat getting off-platform or into private space as automatic trust or consent.

## Handle sexual content and records

Match sexual tone to established reciprocity and current agreement. Account for age, privacy, coercion, exposure, impersonation, unwanted images, stored records, and future discovery where relevant to the story.

Preserve intentional deception or danger when requested, but write the recipient as a perceiving participant rather than programmable audience.

## Deliver the exchange

Return:

- channel assumptions and each character's messaging baseline;
- the message sequence;
- intended and plausible alternate readings;
- timing and silence without automatic conclusions;
- invitation, boundary, repair, or exit points;
- records and continuity consequences.

Do not assign the player's messages, attraction, arousal, consent, response, or next action.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
