# Design character-specific courtship

Build pursuit from two or more changing perspectives, not from a technique that guarantees attraction.

**Action:** Map each participant's want, fear, attention, uncertainty, power,
and exit options, then trace bid, interpretation, response, and changed risk.

**Key point:** Courtship can become sex, romance, friendship, rivalry,
rejection, manipulation, or nothing without one route being the correct one.

**Why:** Reciprocal uncertainty creates chemistry while preserving agency.

## Narrative intention and fictional genre logic

Courtship may be tender, manipulative, coercive, comic, tragic, erotic, or
surreal according to the premise. Preserve the route's intended tension and
consequences without importing dating advice, guaranteed attraction, or a
healthy romantic endpoint.

## Establish each participant

For every important participant, define:

- what they want, fear, and do not yet know;
- acceptable, tempting, and unacceptable outcomes;
- what attracts their attention and what they distrust;
- experience, confidence, social role, and tolerance for exposure;
- cultural, historical, family, gender, and relationship-form scripts;
- power, safety, reputation, privacy, and practical exit options;
- public presentation versus private intention.

Do not infer one person's preferences from sex, gender, sexuality, archetype, or status.

## Build the bid-response sequence

Use:

**Notice → bid → interpretation → response → updated risk → next bid, pause, change, or exit.**

A bid may be a greeting, joke, compliment, invitation, disclosure, disagreement, favour, message, or proposed plan. Make each move change information, risk, or available options. Responses may encourage one kind of closeness while refusing another.

## Separate attraction channels

Map which forms of attraction are present and which are absent or delayed:

- aesthetic or sensory interest;
- sexual desire or arousal;
- romantic attachment or imagined future;
- intellectual, emotional, playful, or creative fascination;
- status, competence, danger, novelty, familiarity, or felt safety;
- desire to be chosen, witnessed, challenged, rescued, or controlled.

These channels may reinforce one another, conflict, or develop at different
speeds. Reciprocated interest can increase attention and willingness to
continue, but it does not prove compatibility, commitment, consent to a later
act, or a destined relationship. Responsive desire may emerge from a chosen
context without being present at the first encounter.

## Choose the approach style

Fit initiation to character and setting: direct, formal, playful, cautious, practical, mediated, competitive, awkward, anonymous, or apparently accidental. Account for audience, law, class, disability, work, distance, technology, reputation, and danger.

Let the setting actively shape the courtship rather than decorate it. A town,
workplace, household, institution, travel route, magical rule, or social taboo
can create proximity, privacy, delay, surveillance, opportunity, or unequal
risk. Treat these pressures as genre and relationship conditions, not as a
guarantee that either character will reciprocate.

Avoid canned openers unless memorisation is intentional characterisation. A successful approach opens an interaction; it does not prove attraction.

Treat seduction frameworks and “love language” models as tools a character may
believe in, perform, resist, misunderstand, or weaponise. A rigid believer may
withhold affection because a partner used the “wrong” modality; a seducer may
freeze when a target does not follow the expected script; a partner may use the
framework to name a real need without accepting its categories as law. The
framework should generate conflict and interpretation, never guarantee
compliance or attraction.

## Use dates to reveal compatibility

Show choices about planning, money, punctuality, accessibility, attention, disclosure, alcohol, sex, privacy, public behaviour, boundaries, and aftermath. Let chemistry coexist with incompatible goals, values, schedules, or relationship forms.

Track what changes after each encounter: expectation, trust, fantasy, evidence, embarrassment, obligation, leverage, or willingness to meet again.

## Handle uncertainty and refusal

Treat delay, cancellation, mixed interest, changed consent, and rejection as new story conditions. Characters may accept, clarify, preserve face, withdraw, bargain, retaliate, or misread, but persistence does not become romantic merely because it later succeeds.

Consent to one action does not imply the next. Preserve the other person's current choices and memory.

## Deliver the courtship map

Return:

- participants' goals, risks, and scripts;
- attraction and uncertainty from each viewpoint;
- bid-response progression;
- date or encounter beats;
- compatibility discoveries and mismatches;
- refusal, exit, and change points;
- continuity consequences and scene hooks.

Preserve intentional darkness. Do not assign the player's attraction, arousal, consent, message, speech, action, relationship goal, or decision.
