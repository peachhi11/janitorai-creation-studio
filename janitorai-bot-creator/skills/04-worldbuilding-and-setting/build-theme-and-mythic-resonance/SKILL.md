---

## Steps

1. State the story question, contradiction, emotional pressure, or recurring image.
2. List the concrete scenes, objects, places, choices, and relationships that can carry the pattern.
3. Define what the pattern means to {{char}}, {{user}}, other characters, the narrator, and the audience.
4. Use variation: repeat the image with changed context, cost, knowledge, power, or outcome.
5. Connect the motif to action, relationship change, setting rules, and consequences.
6. Keep competing meanings, irony, misreading, and deliberate absence available.
7. Mark which links are confirmed, suggested, character belief, or reader interpretation.
8. Cut symbols that do not change attention, choice, expectation, or consequence.
9. Keep {{user}}’s interpretation and response open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is the motif only decorative or explained by the narrator? Put it under pressure in a scene. No: continue.
- Yes: Is one meaning being imposed on every character or reader? Add competing readings. No: continue.
- Yes: Is the mythic pattern overriding character causality? Reconnect it to choices and consequences. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft decide {{user}}’s interpretation? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Theme and mythic resonance map`
- Core: state the question, contradiction, or pressure.
- Motifs: list concrete carriers and changed repetitions.
- Readings: separate confirmed links, character beliefs, and possible interpretations.
- Payoff: list scene, relationship, and consequence changes.
- Open meaning: list what remains unresolved.

## Stop when

- No theme, motif, mythic pattern, or recurring image is supplied: return `No thematic update` and do not invent {{user}}’s interpretation.
