name: build-story-driven-worlds
description: Use when building or revising a fictional setting around the people and scenes who will use it. Trigger phrases include “build only the useful world,” “turn the premise into roleplay,” and “what setting facts matter now?”
---

## Steps

1. State who experiences the world, what they want, what helps or obstructs them, and what genre or mood the setting promises.
2. Mark which facts must be settled now and which can remain open.
3. Choose one charged place, relationship, institution, custom, resource, or disruption as the working centre.
4. For each planned scene, define access, ordinary expectations, knowledge, resources, rules, dangers, opportunities, and relevant social differences.
5. Link important geography, law, religion, magic, technology, work, kinship, class, and naming choices to at least two other elements.
6. Use setting to create local goals, obstacles, rewards, obligations, debts, privileges, disagreement, and later consequences.
7. Trace rule or expectation → burden or access → character tactic → witness or gatekeeper → immediate result → later opportunity or constraint.
8. Expand only when a scene, character, decision, contradiction, or consequence requires it.
9. Keep {{user}}’s knowledge, interpretation, belief, and response open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is the setting becoming an encyclopaedia or exhaustive questionnaire? Keep only facts that affect the story. No: continue.
- Yes: Is an important world element isolated from material or social consequences? Link it to other elements. No: continue.
- Yes: Is a consequential fact hidden from {{user}}’s character despite reasonable knowledge? Reveal it before the choice. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the setting dictate the protagonist’s response? Present pressure and options instead. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Story-driven world brief`
- Centre: state users, wants, genre promise, and working location or disruption.
- Setting: list only the facts needed for current scenes.
- Pressure: trace rules, access, tactics, witnesses, consequences, and later hooks.
- Open work: list deferred questions and unresolved discoveries.

## Stop when

- No premise, scene, or setting problem is supplied: return `No worldbuilding update` and do not invent {{user}}’s response.
