# Build fictional ecologies

Design organisms as participants in habitats, not isolated entries in a bestiary. Decide how much explanation the story needs; coherent effects matter more than a complete origin story.

**Action:** Define the habitat, organism needs, limits, relationships, and scene-level consequences before adding decorative biology.
**Key point:** Ecological coherence comes from interactions and constraints, not encyclopedic detail.
**Why:** Food, energy, season, and competition make extraordinary life feel situated and dramatically useful.

## Define the habitat

Describe the relevant conditions:

- temperature, water, light, air or medium, seasonality, terrain, and disturbance;
- available energy and nutrients;
- shelter, nesting or germination sites, migration corridors, and barriers;
- ordinary variation, rare extremes, and invented forces;
- changes caused by settlement, farming, industry, magic, war, or climate.

Use a real habitat as a comparison when helpful, then record where the fictional one differs.

## Give each organism a way of life

For every story-relevant species, decide:

- how it obtains energy and water;
- what it eats, absorbs, produces, hunts, grazes, scavenges, or decomposes;
- how it reproduces, develops, disperses, and survives bad seasons;
- where it shelters and how much space it uses;
- what preys on, competes with, parasitises, pollinates, transports, or depends on it;
- which senses and behaviours fit its environment;
- what limits its numbers.

Do not give every detail equal weight. Develop the traits that affect scenes and consequences.

## Place extraordinary creatures carefully

For a monster, magical organism, engineered species, or alien life form, specify the departures from familiar biology and their costs or supporting conditions. Consider:

- rarity and range;
- food or energy demand;
- breeding or creation rate;
- territorial, social, migratory, dormant, or opportunistic behaviour;
- vulnerability, disease, competition, and mortality;
- why it has not consumed, displaced, or transformed everything around it.

Origins may remain unknown or disputed. Keep the current ecological footprint consistent even when the explanation is mysterious.

## Trace ecological relationships

Follow important changes through at least two steps. Removing a predator may alter prey numbers, vegetation, disease, crops, and travel. Introducing a new plant may change soil, fire, pollinators, trade, medicine, and land use.

Avoid treating every relationship as a simple linear chain. Organisms can switch foods, move, adapt behaviourally, or respond differently across seasons and life stages.

## Connect people and ecology

Ask how residents observe, name, avoid, cultivate, hunt, protect, trade, poison, worship, study, or misunderstand local life. Include:

- specialist work and practical knowledge;
- ordinary household precautions;
- competition for land, water, food, and labour;
- domestication, pests, disease vectors, companions, and sacred species;
- unequal exposure to ecological harm and unequal control of useful resources.

People are part of the ecology. Their practices may conserve, reshape, exploit, or repair it without belonging to one moral category.

## Audit the ecology

Check that:

- abundant large predators have enough territory and energy, or a stated alternative;
- plants and prey exist for more than atmosphere or monster food;
- species ranges respond to habitat, barriers, seasons, and disturbance;
- danger affects local infrastructure and behaviour;
- an organism's abilities do not appear only when the plot needs them;
- Earth categories are not forced onto genuinely alien life without explanation;
- no species or people is assigned an inherent moral alignment from biology.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Preserve the setting's internal rules, causal consequences, knowledge boundaries, and player sovereignty. Do not turn fictional darkness, eroticism, coercion, obsession, tragedy, comedy, surrealism, or meta-fiction into a real-world manual or an automatic healthy outcome.
