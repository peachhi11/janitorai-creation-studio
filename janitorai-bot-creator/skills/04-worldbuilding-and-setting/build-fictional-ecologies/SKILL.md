## Steps

1. Define temperature, water, light, terrain, season, disturbance, energy, shelter, barriers, and human or magical changes.
2. For each story-relevant organism, define energy, food, water, reproduction, development, shelter, range, senses, behaviour, and limits.
3. For extraordinary life, state its unusual trait and the cost, condition, rarity, vulnerability, or mortality that supports it.
4. Map predation, competition, parasitism, pollination, transport, dependence, disease, and seasonal shifts.
5. Trace at least two consequences when a species is removed, introduced, engineered, hunted, protected, or moved.
6. Show how residents name, avoid, cultivate, hunt, protect, trade, worship, study, or misunderstand local life.
7. Include unequal exposure to ecological harm and unequal control of useful resources.
8. Keep species behaviour consistent and do not derive morality, culture, or desire from biology.
9. Keep {{user}}’s fear, awe, disgust, attachment, and action open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Does a large predator lack food, territory, or a stated alternative? Add the missing limit. No: continue.
- Yes: Does a species appear only when the plot needs it? Establish range, season, or evidence. No: continue.
- Yes: Is a biological trait being used to assign moral character or culture? Remove that claim. No: continue.
- Yes: Are people outside the ecology? Add work, exposure, resource control, and adaptation. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft decide {{user}}’s response to danger? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Fictional ecology map`
- Habitat: list conditions, resources, barriers, and disturbances.
- Organisms: list needs, relationships, limits, and unusual traits.
- Chain: trace ecological and social consequences.
- Scene use: list travel, work, danger, belief, and survival effects.
- Open response: list what {{user}} can notice, know, and choose.

## Stop when

- No fictional habitat, organism, or ecological change is supplied: return `No ecology update` and do not invent {{user}}’s response.
