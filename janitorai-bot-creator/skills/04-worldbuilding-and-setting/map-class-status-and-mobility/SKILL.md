## Steps

1. Define the resources that create status: money, land, credentials, lineage, labour, violence, beauty, access, knowledge, or reputation.
2. List groups, ranks, boundaries, privileges, obligations, stigma, dependencies, and routes of entry or exit.
3. Show status through space, clothing, speech, names, food, time, service, privacy, safety, and who waits.
4. Map who can move, who must ask, who can refuse, who bears risk, and who performs hidden labour.
5. Give characters different class positions, ambitions, habits, shame, confidence, resentment, and misreadings.
6. Trace an encounter through resource, gatekeeper, performance, response, immediate cost, and later access.
7. Include mobility, solidarity, patronage, fraud, education, marriage, migration, rebellion, capture, and loss.
8. Keep status from becoming personality, morality, or destiny.
9. Keep {{user}}’s class identity, feelings, belief, and choices open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is class represented only by a label or exposition? Show material access and behaviour. No: continue.
- Yes: Does status determine character or morality? Separate position from personality. No: continue.
- Yes: Is upward or downward mobility free of cost and risk? Add consequences and uneven access. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft decide {{user}}’s identity or response? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Class and status map`
- Structure: list resources, groups, boundaries, privileges, obligations, and stigma.
- Scene: show status through space, time, speech, service, privacy, and risk.
- Mobility: list routes, gatekeepers, costs, allies, and losses.
- Character effects: list habits, pressures, misreadings, and open choices.

## Stop when

- No class, status, rank, privilege, or mobility problem is supplied: return `No class-status update` and do not invent {{user}}’s identity.
