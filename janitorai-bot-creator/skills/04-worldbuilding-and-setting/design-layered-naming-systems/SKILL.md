# Design layered naming systems

Treat a name as a social and historical object, not a decorative string.

**Action:** Define naming categories, cultural layers, sound rules, routes of
entry, and readability gates before generating examples.
**Key point:** Category determines how a name behaves; layer origin records why
that form exists.
**Why:** Layering keeps a setting from making every name sound as though it
came from the same bucket.

## Separate the name categories

Keep at least these categories distinct:

- **Personal:** light, speakable, and suited to repeated dialogue.
- **House or clan:** heavier historical texture; may preserve lineage, conquest,
  migration, occupation, or founding myths.
- **Title:** signals rank, office, institution, ritual, or achieved role before
  it signals personality.
- **Place:** follows its own sub-types and historical strata rather than simply
  reusing personal-name patterns.

Tag every generated name with `category` and `layer_origin`. Use:

```text
category: personal | house | title | place
layer_origin: core-culture | class/role | frontier
```

Do not let a category tag become a stereotype. It describes the naming system,
not the moral nature of the person or institution bearing the name.

## Build the cultural base

For each culture, record three to five relevant traits such as environment,
values, technology, institutions, contact history, and linguistic neighbours.
Then define separate profiles for personal, house, and title names. Class and
archetype should modify a base profile rather than replace it: a ceremonial
order may lengthen or formalise local forms while remaining recognisably local.

Reserve frontier forms for borderlands, ruins, conquered regions, trade routes,
mixed settlements, and other contact zones. A frontier loosens phonotactic
constraints only by a stated margin and must have a route of entry: migration,
trade, prestige borrowing, imposed administration, religious conversion,
intermarriage, or an older substrate.

## Give places their own history

Use sub-types with different tendencies:

- continents: broad, old, and geographically sweeping;
- kingdoms: shaped by ruling power, resource, or political claim;
- settlements: often descriptive, practical, commemorative, or administrative;
- rivers, peaks, and other features: likely to preserve older linguistic strata.

Record, where useful, the current form, earlier form, language or community of
origin, meaning at the time of naming, later reinterpretations, and who uses
which form now. A place can carry several layers at once: an older river name,
a later city name, and a conquering fortress name need not share one grammar.
Do not erase local or older names merely because an official form exists.

## Apply dual readability gates

Every candidate must pass both tests:

1. **Silent readability:** a reader can parse it without tripping on the page.
2. **Aloud readability:** it can be spoken cleanly by a narrator, audiobook
   reader, game master, or player.

Reject or revise stray apostrophes, ambiguous vowel runs, unsupported
consonant clusters, accidental real-world insults, and forms whose spelling
does not support the intended pronunciation. Preserve deliberate difficulty
only when the setting gives the reader a usable pronunciation route.

## Audit the system

For each proposed name, check:

- category and layer origin are explicit;
- the form follows the appropriate profile rather than the global pool;
- class or role changes are traceable and not arbitrary;
- frontier loosenings have a historical route of entry;
- place names reflect terrain, use, power, and temporal strata where relevant;
- silent and aloud readability both pass;
- meanings are not being retrofitted as false certainty;
- different communities may preserve, shorten, translate, mock, or contest the
  name;
- the naming system adds social and narrative consequence without dictating
  character identity, desire, or player choice.

When the source material is incomplete, mark invented etymology, uncertain
pronunciation, and unresolved historical layers as open rather than presenting
them as canon.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Preserve the setting's internal rules, causal consequences, knowledge boundaries, and player sovereignty. Do not turn fictional darkness, eroticism, coercion, obsession, tragedy, comedy, surrealism, or meta-fiction into a real-world manual or an automatic healthy outcome.
