---

## Steps

1. Define the language layers, periods, communities, institutions, and contact zones that shaped the names.
2. Separate personal names, house names, titles, occupational names, place names, nicknames, and outsider labels.
3. Define who chooses, grants, inherits, translates, shortens, rejects, or changes each form.
4. Map pronunciation, spelling, register, gender or status signals, taboo, intimacy, and deliberate misnaming.
5. Record older linguistic layers that survive after rulers, populations, or languages change.
6. Use names in scenes where they affect access, respect, memory, threat, belonging, or misunderstanding.
7. Give {{char}} and {{user}} different knowledge and reasons for using or avoiding each form.
8. Keep invented etymology separate from documented source material.
9. Keep {{user}}’s preferred name, identity, interpretation, and response open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is every name generated from one language or one social group? Add layers and contact history. No: continue.
- Yes: Does a name signal identity or status without a character choosing that meaning? Make the use contextual. No: continue.
- Yes: Is a real culture being copied as a decorative naming bank? State provenance and change the design. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft decide {{user}}’s name or response to misnaming? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Layered naming system`
- Layers: list languages, periods, communities, and contact zones.
- Forms: list name categories, users, meanings, and register.
- Scene use: list access, status, intimacy, conflict, and memory effects.
- Examples: provide names with stated source or invention status.
- Open choice: list forms {{user}} may accept, reject, or change.

## Stop when

- No naming problem, culture, character, or place is supplied: return `No naming update` and do not invent {{user}}’s preferred name.
