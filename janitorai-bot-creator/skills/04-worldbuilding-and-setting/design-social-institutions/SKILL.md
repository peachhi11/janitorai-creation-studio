## Steps

1. State the institution’s purpose, jurisdiction, history, public story, material resources, and beneficiaries.
2. Map members, ranks, roles, gatekeepers, workers, outsiders, dependants, and people who can leave.
3. Define formal rules, ordinary procedures, informal customs, exceptions, sanctions, records, and appeals.
4. Show who controls time, space, money, information, care, credentials, safety, reputation, or force.
5. Give institutional actors different incentives, limits, loyalties, fears, and personal views.
6. Trace request → rule → discretion → response → immediate cost or benefit → later record or relationship.
7. Include workarounds, corruption, mutual aid, resistance, capture, reform, decay, and unintended effects.
8. Connect the institution to kinship, class, belief, geography, economy, technology, and daily life.
9. Keep {{user}}’s beliefs, consent, and choices open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is the institution only a label or backdrop? Add concrete people, resources, and procedures. No: continue.
- Yes: Are official rules the same as lived practice? Add exceptions, enforcement, and workarounds. No: continue.
- Yes: Is every official identical? Give them varied incentives and limits. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the institution decide {{user}}’s response? Present options and costs instead. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Social institution map`
- Purpose: state history, jurisdiction, public story, resources, and beneficiaries.
- Structure: list roles, ranks, access, rules, procedures, exceptions, sanctions, and appeals.
- Scene chain: trace requests, gatekeepers, discretion, results, and records.
- Change: list workarounds, resistance, reform, capture, and unintended effects.
- Player space: list known options without choosing {{user}}’s action.

## Stop when

- No fictional institution or social system is supplied: return `No institution update` and do not invent {{user}}’s response.
