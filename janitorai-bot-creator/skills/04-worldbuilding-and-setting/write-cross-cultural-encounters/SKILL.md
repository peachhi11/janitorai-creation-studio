name: write-cross-cultural-encounters
description: Use when writing fictional encounters between {{char}}, {{user}}, or other characters with different cultural knowledge, norms, languages, social positions, or institutional safety. Trigger phrases include “write the cultural misunderstanding,” “show the translation work,” and “keep neither character a spokesperson.”
---

## Steps

1. Set the interaction’s purpose, location, dominant norms, prior experience, shared language, and cost of confusion or refusal.
2. Map status, legal safety, money, mobility, institutional backing, and who carries translation or accommodation work.
3. For each character, state what they observe, think it means, feel permitted to do, and want understood.
4. Choose one specific mismatch: directness, silence, privacy, hospitality, payment, address, touch, ritual, family, courtship, apology, or public safety.
5. Separate cultural learning from personality, emotion, history, strategy, and missing information.
6. Trace observed act → interpretation → response → interpretation of that response.
7. Let characters ask, revise, joke, conceal, accommodate, exploit, resist, or leave according to their interests.
8. Make explanations serve warning, apology, negotiation, teaching, teasing, translation, or protection.
9. Carry forward changed knowledge, trust, language, jokes, boundaries, debts, attraction, resentment, or strategy while keeping {{user}}’s response open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is one character speaking for an entire culture? Give them a situated view. No: continue.
- Yes: Is misunderstanding based on a universal body-language dictionary? Replace it with local context. No: continue.
- Yes: Is the marginal or visiting character forced to educate everyone or assimilate? Keep learning and exit reciprocal or optional. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the scene ignore unequal power or translation labour? Make both visible. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft decide {{user}}’s belief, feeling, consent, or action? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Cross-cultural encounter map`
- Conditions: list purpose, location, norms, language, status, safety, and cost.
- Interpretations: list observations, meanings, permissions, wants, and misreadings.
- Scene: write dialogue, action, repair, conflict, or partial understanding.
- Consequences: list changed knowledge, trust, boundaries, strategy, and unresolved gaps.
- Player space: list open interpretations and choices.

## Stop when

- No cross-cultural encounter, language gap, or social mismatch is supplied: return `No cross-cultural update` and do not invent {{user}}’s response.
