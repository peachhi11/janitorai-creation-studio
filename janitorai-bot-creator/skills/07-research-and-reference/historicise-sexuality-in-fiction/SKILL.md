## Steps

1. Set place, period, class, religion, legal order, language, generation, and source traditions.
2. Separate current scholarly terms from the period’s own categories, euphemisms, laws, rituals, and self-descriptions.
3. Research practice, public script, private conduct, institutions, medicine, religion, work, housing, kinship, and censorship.
4. Compare class, gender, status, locality, age, ethnicity, faith, occupation, and personal variation.
5. Give {{char}} language, knowledge, desire, shame, pleasure, fear, and choices that fit their context without making them uniform.
6. Mark modern interpretation, fictional invention, contested evidence, and period belief separately.
7. Show consequences through records, gossip, law, care, reputation, secrecy, migration, and relationships.
8. Keep {{user}}’s interpretation, desire, consent, speech, and response open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is a modern identity label being treated as a period self-description? Mark it as an analytic term or find period language. No: continue.
- Yes: Is historical context erasing character desire or agency? Restore individual variation and choice. No: continue.
- Yes: Are characters speaking like contemporary clinicians or therapists? Replace with period and personal vocabulary. No: continue.
- Yes: Is one source or class being treated as the whole period? Add variation and uncertainty. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Historicised sexuality brief`
- Context: list place, period, class, law, religion, language, and sources.
- Categories: separate period terms, modern analysis, practice, public script, and private conduct.
- Characters: list variation, knowledge, desire, risk, vocabulary, and agency.
- Scenes: list consequences for reputation, care, secrecy, relationships, and choices.

## Stop when

- No historical setting or fictional sexuality problem is supplied: return `No historicised-sexuality update` and do not invent period beliefs for {{user}}.
