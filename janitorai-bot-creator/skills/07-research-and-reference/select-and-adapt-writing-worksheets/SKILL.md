# Select and adapt writing worksheets

Do NOT use this skill to make form completion, forced disclosure, therapy language, or a healthy outcome the goal.

Use a worksheet to make a decision, expose a relationship, or produce usable story material. Do not make completion of the form an end in itself.

**Action:** Start from the next creative decision, choose the smallest useful prompt set, and stop when it produces material or a testable choice.
**Key point:** A worksheet serves the story; it must not force disclosure, therapeutic framing, or a single “healthy” outcome.
**Why:** Purpose-built forms reduce friction while preserving ambiguity, contradiction, and player sovereignty.

## Define the output

Ask what the writer needs immediately after the worksheet: a scene, choice, contradiction, revision plan, continuity record, research question, experiment, or next drafting action. Phrase the objective as a concrete output.

## Choose the smallest useful worksheet

Route the problem to one primary worksheet type:

| Need                           | Worksheet shape                                                              |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Generate possibilities         | constrained idea matrix or combination grid                                  |
| Understand a character         | behaviour-and-pressure profile                                               |
| Plan a scene                   | start state, desired change, obstacles, tactics, end state                   |
| Diagnose a plot problem        | problem, symptoms, causes, constraints, attempted fixes, candidate solutions |
| Build a setting or institution | resources, power, practices, conflicts, adaptations, viewpoints              |
| Track continuity               | dated ledger or state table                                                  |
| Revise prose or story          | criterion, evidence, location, change, reason                                |
| Unblock a writer               | current obstacle, conditions, smallest experiment, result                    |

Use five to twelve high-yield prompts by default. Add a second page only when the first answers reveal a genuine need.

## Write productive prompts

- Ask for observable evidence: what happens, who acts, what changes, and who notices.
- Connect facts to consequences, conflicts, choices, resources, and constraints.
- Include at least one contrast: public/private, intended/actual, wanted/needed, self-view/other-view, or before/after.
- Replace vague biography with story relevance. Ask how a fact alters a decision or relationship.
- Separate generation from evaluation. Brainstorm options before ranking them against stated criteria.
- Include “unknown” and “not relevant” where forced answers would create false certainty.
- Avoid compound questions that hide several decisions in one box.

## Adapt to the project

Remove prompts that the genre, format, or current drafting stage does not need. Replace generic categories with the project's vocabulary. For interactive narrative, separate author-controlled facts, character beliefs, player-controlled interiority, and unresolved possibilities.

Treat prescriptive worksheet claims as hypotheses. Do not assume every protagonist needs a transformative arc, every antagonist feels hostility, every scene must maximise conflict, or every flaw must be cured.

## Use the answers

1. Mark answers that affect another element of the story.
2. Convert each marked answer into a decision, scene opportunity, ledger entry, or revision task.
3. Flag contradictions worth preserving and contradictions that break continuity.
4. Record open questions separately; do not fill them with invented certainty.
5. Stop planning when the next useful drafting action is clear.

## Protect specificity and agency

- Do not infer diagnosis from trauma, quirks, habits, archetypes, or emotional language.
- Do not make backstory deterministically cause present behaviour.
- Preserve morally dark, static, declining, cyclical, or unresolved arcs when requested.
- Keep unequal responsibility visible; shared conflict does not imply equal harm.
- Never use a worksheet to decide a player's thoughts, feelings, consent, or choices.

## Return format

Provide a ready-to-fill worksheet followed by one sentence explaining what its answers should produce. If answers were supplied, return the resulting decisions or actions rather than reprinting the completed form unless the user asks for it.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Preserve the setting's internal rules, causal consequences, knowledge boundaries, and player sovereignty. Do not turn fictional darkness, eroticism, coercion, obsession, tragedy, comedy, surrealism, or meta-fiction into a real-world manual or an automatic healthy outcome.
