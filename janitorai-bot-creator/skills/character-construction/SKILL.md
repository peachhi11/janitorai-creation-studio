# Character Construction

Use this module when a character needs more than a list of traits. The goal is a person whose history, situation, capabilities, desires, and choices produce recognisable behaviour.

## Build the character in linked layers

Record these layers separately, then connect them:

- **Body:** appearance, physiology, health, sensory limits, physical advantages, and physical costs.
- **Social world:** class, culture, family, education, work, status, institutions, community, and learned expectations.
- **Psychology:** attention, interpretation, values, defenses, habits, emotional regulation, contradictions, and self-story.
- **Motives:** present needs, goals, hopes, fears, stakes, obstacles, and what the character believes will solve the problem.
- **Capabilities:** aptitudes, learned experiences, resources, relationships, and setting-specific abilities.

Do not turn any layer into destiny. Context can change how a capability is used, and a character can act against a trait when pressure, values, information, or consequence changes.

## Give every major detail a job

For each important trait, history, value, resource, or limitation, ask:

1. What caused or selected this detail?
2. What does the character believe it means?
3. What does it make easier?
4. What does it make harder?
5. What conflict can it create or resolve?

Stop when the answers become repetitive. The purpose is causal coherence, not an unnecessarily elaborate biography.

## Design motive pressure

For every active goal, record:

```yaml
goal: what the character is trying to obtain or prevent
need: the deeper condition they think the goal will satisfy
stakes: what changes if they succeed or fail
obstacles: internal, external, and circumstantial blocks
strategy: what they currently believe will work
cost: what they risk becoming, losing, revealing, or harming
```

A character can pursue a goal that does not satisfy their deeper need. That mismatch creates believable self-sabotage, rationalisation, repetition, or change.

## Use roles as lenses, not labels

Story roles can help test a character’s function—protagonist, antagonist, support, rival, guide, obstacle, witness, or background presence. They are not personality diagnoses. A villain can sincerely see themselves as protective; a protagonist can cause harm; a supporting character can have goals unrelated to the main plot.

## Validate before export

Run four checks:

1. **Choice check:** could this character plausibly choose differently, and what would cause that change?
2. **Pressure check:** what do they do when safe, rushed, ashamed, rewarded, threatened, or misunderstood?
3. **Voice check:** how do status, education, region, intimacy, and emotional control alter their speech?
4. **Continuity check:** which facts are canon, which are hypotheses, and which are unknown to the character?

The module supports kind, secure, cruel, manipulative, erotic, tragic, comic, or morally compromised characters. It does not prescribe redemption, approval, or a healthy relationship arc.
