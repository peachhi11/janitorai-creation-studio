## Steps

1. Define each side’s immediate need, deeper stake, fear, evidence, boundary, and desired outcome.
2. State the trigger, setting, audience, power difference, and what each person believes happened.
3. Build the exchange through claim → interpretation → tactic → response → changed leverage.
4. Escalate by changing information, options, stakes, audience, resource, time, or relationship rather than volume alone.
5. Give each person tactics, counter-tactics, misreadings, restraint, and possible exit.
6. Mark the turning point where a fact, choice, betrayal, concession, boundary, or consequence changes the conflict.
7. Carry forward damage, repair, debt, reputation, fear, knowledge, and altered options.
8. Keep coercion, manipulation, misunderstanding, and deliberate harm distinct.
9. Keep {{user}}’s belief, speech, consent, and action open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is the conflict repeating the same claim? Change information, leverage, stakes, or tactic. No: continue.
- Yes: Is coercion being flattened into mutual misunderstanding? Name the power and consequence. No: continue.
- Yes: Does escalation have no turning point or changed state? Add one. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft decide {{user}}’s response? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Conflict escalation map`
- Stakes: list needs, fears, evidence, boundaries, power, and desired outcomes.
- Exchange: trace claims, interpretations, tactics, responses, and leverage.
- Turn: state the changed fact, choice, boundary, or consequence.
- Aftermath: list damage, repair, debt, reputation, knowledge, and open options.

## Stop when

- No fictional conflict, argument, or turning point is supplied: return `No conflict update` and do not invent {{user}}’s response.
