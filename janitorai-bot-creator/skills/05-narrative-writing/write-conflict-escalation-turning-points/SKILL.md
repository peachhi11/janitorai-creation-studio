# Write conflict escalation and turning points

Do NOT use this skill to force reconciliation, mutual misunderstanding, or a morally tidy resolution.

Begin with the concrete issue, then expose the meaning underneath it. Conflict is not automatically loud: it can be delay, politeness, logistics, withdrawal, seduction, silence, public performance, or a decision made without consultation.

**Action:** Escalate through changed stakes, tactics, information, and power until a decision makes the old arrangement impossible.
**Key point:** Escalation need not resolve conflict cleanly or reward the most “healthy” choice.
**Why:** Specific turning points preserve character agency and support tragic, toxic, erotic, or unresolved arcs.

## Build the sequence

1. Establish each person’s current state, stake, and private need.
2. Give the initiating cue and each person’s first interpretation.
3. Choose tactics: ask, explain, bargain, deflect, joke, accuse, test, placate, threaten, leave, or enlist an audience.
4. Escalate through a changed meaning, not just louder volume: a boundary is crossed, a secret is exposed, a motive is misread, or a power difference becomes undeniable.
5. Mark the turning point: recognition, refusal, revelation, consequence, outside interruption, or a choice to continue.
6. End with a new condition and residue; do not reset the relationship because the scene ends.

Keep the user’s agency open. Separate ordinary disagreement from manipulation, coercion, intimidation, and abuse. If power is unequal, do not force symmetrical blame or call compliance a resolution.

## Deliverable

Return a conflict beat sheet: issue, private meaning, tactic, counter-tactic, escalation, power shift, turning point, immediate outcome, and unresolved residue.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
