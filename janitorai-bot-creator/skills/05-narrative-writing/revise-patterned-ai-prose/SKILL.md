# Revise patterned AI prose

Do NOT use this skill to enforce universal style bans or erase deliberate character voice, erotic charge, or roughness.

Treat pattern lists as editorial heuristics, not universal bans or proof of authorship. A construction used once may be effective. Revise only when repetition, mismatch, or accumulation damages the requested voice.

**Action:** Diagnose repeated habits against the requested voice, then make the smallest edits that restore specificity and rhythm.
**Key point:** Removing a pattern is not automatically an improvement; preserve deliberate stylisation, darkness, erotic charge, and character idiolect.
**Why:** Context-sensitive revision avoids replacing one generic voice with another.

## Establish the target voice

Identify audience, genre, viewpoint, register, desired roughness, and any user-provided style constraints. Preserve intentional fragments, repetition, rhetorical questions, ornament, slang, dialect, and disfluency when they belong to the speaker or form.

## Diagnose by accumulation

Mark repeated habits without editing yet:

- **Manufactured emphasis:** negative parallelism, short fragments, rule-of-three chains, anaphora, self-answered questions, suspense fillers, or quotable one-liners.
- **Structural padding:** preambles, reasoning narration, premise stacking, repeated summaries, overlong conclusions, excessive enumeration, tie-backs, or duplicated content.
- **Generic authority:** vague attributions, appeals to familiarity, inflated stakes, promotional language, rapid historical analogies, or superficial significance clauses.
- **Lexical mannerism:** favourite adverbs, synonym cycling, invented analytical labels, ornate stock nouns, pompous substitutes for simple verbs, or fashionable filler phrases.
- **Formatting mannerism:** unnecessary title case, bold-first bullets, decorative symbols, or repetitive question-based headings.
- **Forced explanation:** patronising analogies, strained figurative language, false ranges, token acknowledgements of difficulty, or several paragraphs diluting one point.

Prioritise clusters. One em dash or one triad is not a defect; several conspicuous habits competing in a short passage usually warrant revision.

## Apply a construction audit without flattening voice

When using a banned-construction list, classify each hit before changing it:

- **incidental pattern:** remove or reshape if it adds padding or generic emphasis;
- **character pattern:** retain when the repetition is a tic, ritual, defence,
  manipulation, obsession, or voice marker;
- **genre pattern:** retain or calibrate when the convention carries erotic,
  romantic, comic, gothic, or melodramatic promise;
- **structural pattern:** keep when it controls pacing, refrain, escalation, or
  deliberate narrative symmetry.

Then test the smallest revision against four questions: Is the meaning still
intact? Is the speaker or narrator still recognisable? Has the scene lost useful
friction or erotic charge? Did the edit accidentally turn a dark or unreliable
voice into neutral therapeutic prose? Stop when the answer is good enough; do
not optimise until every rough edge has disappeared.

## Revise in passes

1. **Meaning:** write the passage's indispensable claims or story beats in plain notes. Keep factual qualifications and character intent.
2. **Structure:** cut announced intentions, repeated premises, duplicate summaries, and conclusions that merely restate the body. Move the answer or dramatic action to the front when appropriate.
3. **Evidence:** replace vague authority and inflated consensus with a named source, an observable detail, a bounded attribution, or no attribution.
4. **Language:** reuse the stable noun for one referent; prefer direct verbs; remove ornamental modifiers that add no distinction.
5. **Rhythm:** combine needless fragments, vary sentence length according to content, and break conspicuous templates. Do not manufacture variety by cycling synonyms.
6. **Voice:** restore details, syntax, humour, abrasiveness, hesitation, or intensity particular to the author or character.
7. **Stop:** compare with the original and end the edit when clarity and specificity improve without loss of meaning or voice.

When revising AI-assisted text, compare the baseline, the proposed revision,
and the accepted version. Check whether a gain in novelty or variation also
introduced theme drift, structural template language, emotional convergence,
or a loss of character idiolect. Keep a useful oddity when it belongs to the
genre or speaker; do not optimise toward a model's average notion of fluency.

For sentence-level craft, treat punctuation, sentence length, active or passive
voice, dialogue tags, paragraph breaks, figurative language, and word choice as
rhythm and viewpoint decisions. Correct an error when it obscures meaning or
violates the requested house style; retain a marked construction when it carries
voice, emphasis, dialect, intimacy, menace, comedy, or deliberate awkwardness.

## Protect fiction and roleplay

- Do not turn all dialogue into neutral professional prose.
- Distinguish narrator habits from character habits; a repetitive phrase may be a deliberate tell, defence, ritual, or stress response.
- Preserve unreliable, manipulative, obsessive, coercive, grandiose, or self-deceiving language when it performs character work.
- Keep player sovereignty: revise non-player narration and speech without assigning the player's thoughts, feelings, consent, or choices.
- Retain knowledge boundaries and biased interpretations inside the correct viewpoint.

## Return useful edits

When the user asks for revision, provide the revised text first. Add a short change note only when it helps them understand a consequential choice. Do not surround a concise edit with the same preambles and summaries the skill is intended to remove.

If a requested construction is part of the author's voice, keep it. If the user wants a strict house-style pass, apply that standard consistently and state any trade-off that materially changes tone or characterisation.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
