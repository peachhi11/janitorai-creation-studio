# Write subtextual dialogue

Give each line a reason to be spoken now, and let the surface wording and the
private pressure differ when that creates useful tension.

**Action:** Establish the speaker's immediate want, tactic, knowledge, voice,
and risk; write the line; then test the reply, implication, and changed power.

**Key point:** Subtext is not a puzzle. The reader should be able to feel the
pressure even when the speaker avoids naming it.

**Why:** Dialogue becomes character-specific action rather than an information
delivery system.

## Build the exchange

For each turn, record:

- immediate objective and concealed concern;
- what the speaker believes the other person knows;
- tactic: ask, test, bargain, tease, evade, reassure, threaten, confess, or
  perform;
- status, relationship history, audience, and practical stakes;
- the line's surface meaning, implication, and possible misreading;
- what changes after the line.

Let dialogue do more than one job where possible: reveal character while
advancing action, changing knowledge, exposing a relationship, or altering
power. Use action beats and silence only when they add information a viewpoint
character could notice.

## Protect voice and agency

Differentiate speakers through syntax, vocabulary, rhythm, humour, avoidance,
metaphor, formality, and what they refuse to say. Do not give every character
the same polished wit or therapeutic vocabulary.

Keep necessary directness. A character may say exactly what they mean when
they are blunt, exhausted, ritualised, honest, inexperienced, or trying to
control the exchange.

In interactive fiction, write the AI-controlled character's words and leave the
player's reply, interiority, consent, and physical response unresolved.

## Fictional genre logic

Subtext follows the story's genre, culture, relationship, and narrative aim.
Dark, erotic, comic, tragic, manipulative, or surreal dialogue may pursue its
intended effect without being converted into a healthy communication lesson.
Keep the consequences and knowledge boundaries legible rather than making a
line universally persuasive.

## Deliverable

Return a dialogue beat sheet or revised exchange with speaker objective,
tactic, surface line, subtext, response pressure, voice notes, and the changed
relationship or information state.
