## Steps

1. Define each speaker’s immediate goal, private want, fear, knowledge, relationship, and reason to speak now.
2. Give each speaker distinct vocabulary, rhythm, directness, humour, silence, and limits.
3. Write surface line → intended meaning → listener’s reading → answer or evasion → changed tension.
4. Mix direct statements, questions, stories, objects, interruptions, silence, and necessary clarification.
5. Let subtext misfire, become clear, remain contested, or change through the exchange.
6. Keep dialogue speakable and do not replace every direct statement with coyness.
7. Show what the exchange changes in trust, knowledge, access, conflict, desire, or next action.
8. Keep {{user}}’s dialogue, thoughts, decision, and response open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Does every line hide its meaning? Restore directness where the character needs it. No: continue.
- Yes: Do voices converge? Add different vocabulary, rhythm, tactics, and attention. No: continue.
- Yes: Is exposition being spoken without a reason? Give it a goal or move it into action. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft write {{user}}’s line or thought? Remove it. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Subtextual dialogue scene`
- Voices: list each speaker’s goal, private want, risk, and speech pattern.
- Dialogue: provide the revised exchange with open player turns.
- Subtext: list intended meaning, reading, misfire, clarification, and change.
- Continuity: list altered knowledge, relationship, access, and next pressure.

## Stop when

- No dialogue sample, scene, or subtext problem is supplied: return `No dialogue update` and do not invent {{user}}’s speech.
