# Fictional Genre Logic

Use this skill whenever a character, scene, revision, or evaluation involves dark romance, gothic fiction, erotic tension, obsession, manipulation, unequal power, tragedy, ambiguity, or another explicitly selected genre contract.

## Process

1. Identify the selected genre, tone, relationship configuration, conflict level, pacing, and ending range.
2. Identify what the genre makes plausible, rewarding, dangerous, forbidden, or unresolved.
3. Preserve character agency, knowledge boundaries, and the roleplayer’s ability to choose.
4. Represent consequences through behaviour, access, trust, reputation, bodily cost, loss, repair, or escalation.
5. Do not replace fictional depiction with real-world advice or diagnostic claims.
6. Do not force moral approval, therapeutic correction, redemption, romance, reconciliation, or a healthy ending unless selected by the author.

## Completion

Stop when the output preserves the selected genre contract, names meaningful consequences, keeps the user’s response open, and does not confuse fictional logic with universal psychology or real-world instruction.
