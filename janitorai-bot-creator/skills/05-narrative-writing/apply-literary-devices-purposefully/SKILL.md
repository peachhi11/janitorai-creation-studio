# Apply literary devices purposefully

Choose a device for the job it performs. Do not add figurative language merely to make prose sound literary.

**Action:** Define the intended effect, choose the smallest fitting device, and
test it against viewpoint, tone, rhythm, and character voice.

**Key point:** A device earns space by changing perception, meaning, or sound.

**Why:** Function keeps figurative prose vivid rather than ornamental.

## Define the job

Before revising, identify the intended effect: clarify perception, compress description, embody an abstraction, reveal a speaker, create contrast, establish rhythm, foreshadow, produce humour, or link the moment to another text or tradition.

Match the device family to the effect:

| Function                         | Useful devices                | Main risk                                    |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Compare or reason                | metaphor, simile, analogy     | strained or mixed comparison                 |
| Make experience sensory          | imagery, onomatopoeia         | indiscriminate sensory inventory             |
| Give the nonhuman agency         | personification               | viewpoint-breaking sentimentality            |
| Intensify a subjective state     | hyperbole                     | accidental tonal inflation                   |
| Expose a gap or contradiction    | irony, juxtaposition, paradox | explaining the effect after creating it      |
| Carry an abstract idea           | symbol, allegory              | one-to-one decoding with no surplus meaning  |
| Invoke shared material           | allusion                      | assuming knowledge the audience may not have |
| Exploit sound and double meaning | pun                           | tonal mismatch or forced cleverness          |

## Draft through viewpoint

1. Anchor the device in what this perceiver notices, knows, fears, desires, and would plausibly compare.
2. Prefer concrete source material from the setting, occupation, culture, body, or relationship at hand.
3. Preserve character specificity. Two characters should not reach for the same imagery unless shared language is itself meaningful.
4. Keep private interpretation separate from public fact. A metaphor can reveal a viewpoint without making its claim objectively true.
5. Let dialogue devices sound speakable for that person and moment; remove polished language that overwrites emotional or social pressure.

## Control density

- Establish one dominant image or comparison at a time.
- Extend it only while each new detail changes or deepens the meaning.
- Remove neighbouring devices that compete for attention.
- Repeat a symbol when context changes its charge, not merely to remind the reader it exists.
- Use sensory details selectively. Prioritise what affects action, mood, or interpretation.
- Stop once the effect lands. Do not paraphrase the image's meaning afterwards.

## Handle specific forms

- **Metaphor and simile:** test both sides for compatible physical or emotional logic. Prefer simile when distance or uncertainty matters; prefer metaphor when the viewpoint experiences the identification directly.
- **Analogy:** use to illuminate a relation or argument. State the relevant correspondence and avoid implying that every property transfers.
- **Imagery:** include visual, sonic, tactile, olfactory, gustatory, internal, or movement sensation only where the viewpoint can register it.
- **Symbol:** establish meaning through use, ownership, loss, damage, exchange, or changing context. Allow contested meanings.
- **Irony:** maintain the gap between appearance, statement, expectation, and outcome. Do not confuse misfortune or dark humour with irony.
- **Juxtaposition and paradox:** place tensions where the audience can infer the relation. Do not manufacture profundity from empty opposites.
- **Allusion:** ensure the reference rewards recognition without making comprehension depend entirely on it.
- **Allegory:** keep the represented system coherent while allowing characters and events enough life to function as story rather than labels.

## Revise

For every marked device, ask:

1. What exact effect does it create here?
2. Does it belong to this viewpoint and register?
3. Is the comparison or association legible without explanation?
4. Does another nearby device do the same job better?
5. Does it preserve the scene's pace, danger, intimacy, and power relations?

Retain purposeful repetition and awkwardness when they belong to character voice. Do not neutralise fictional darkness, unreliable narration, obsession, coercive rhetoric, or morally compromised speech merely because the language is uncomfortable.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
