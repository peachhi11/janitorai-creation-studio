# Write embodied social signals

Make nonverbal behaviour part of the interaction, not an annotation explaining emotion.

**Action:** Anchor viewpoint, baseline, power, observable signal, interpretation,
reciprocal adjustment, and consequence.

**Key point:** Selective physical detail should raise questions, not answer every
inner state.

**Why:** Embodied subtext works when readers can feel the interaction without
being handed a clinical translation.

## Anchor the scene

Identify:

- viewpoint and what that observer can perceive;
- relationship history and current power;
- setting, culture, audience, distance, exits, and obstacles;
- each character's established baseline;
- the beat that changes the interaction.

Use only channels the viewpoint can notice. Do not grant access to hidden motives through a gesture.

## Write a signal sequence

Build the beat as:

**Prompt or change → observable response → adjustment or recovery → observer interpretation → response to that interpretation → consequence.**

The observer may be perceptive, biased, unfamiliar, distracted, or deliberately misled. Phrase interpretation through viewpoint rather than narrator certainty.

## Coordinate channels selectively

Choose two or three channels per beat:

- face and gaze;
- torso, hands, feet, posture, or movement quality;
- proximity, angle, barriers, territory, or exit access;
- touch, refusal, recoil, return, or stillness;
- pitch, pace, volume, rhythm, breath, pause, or silence;
- clothing, grooming, scent, objects, and environmental arrangement.

Let channels align, conflict, or change at different speeds. Incongruence should create a question, not answer it.

## Make the signal relational

Show what the other person does because of the cue: accommodate, mirror, challenge, crowd, retreat, freeze, appease, interrupt, test, ignore, or repair. Track who is permitted to stare, touch, occupy space, remain silent, or misread without consequence.

Attraction can appear through authored reciprocity and accumulated choices. A bodily response is not consent, and interest in one beat is not permission for the next. Treat freezing, compliance under pressure, intoxication, and inability to leave as ambiguous or constrained—not coy agreement.

## Preserve character voice

Keep analytic labels out of prose and ordinary dialogue unless the viewpoint would genuinely use them. Prefer a specific action and consequence over “defensive body language”, “a dominance display”, or “a micro-expression of jealousy”.

Permit deliberate intimidation, deception, erotic presentation, coercive performance, masking, and morally ambiguous misreading when the fiction calls for them. Portray effects and agency without presenting tactics as guaranteed psychology.

## Revise for continuity

Check that:

- the behaviour fits the character's baseline or marks a deliberate change;
- physical capability, pain, sensory load, and environment are respected;
- cultural meaning is researched rather than assumed;
- repeated signals vary with stakes and do not become tics;
- later reactions remember earlier touch, crowding, withdrawal, or misreading;
- player feelings, body, speech, consent, and choices remain unassigned.

## Deliver the scene pass

Return:

- the revised passage or beat sequence;
- a short signal-and-response map;
- viewpoint interpretations marked as inference;
- continuity notes and unresolved ambiguity.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
