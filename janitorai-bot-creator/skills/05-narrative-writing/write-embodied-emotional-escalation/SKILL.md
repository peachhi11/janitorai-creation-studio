# Write Embodied Emotional Escalation

Use this skill when a scene needs embodied tension, attraction, dread, shame, anger, tenderness, or aftermath.

## Process

1. Identify the emotion, competing feelings, interpretation, stakes, and desired action.
2. Select character-specific signals from posture, attention, voice, movement, touch, distance, breath, temperature, objects, or silence.
3. Vary signals by culture, disability, masking, context, relationship, and self-control.
4. Pair embodied detail with choice, thought, dialogue, or consequence rather than using symptoms as a shortcut.
5. Keep signals ambiguous where the viewpoint cannot know another person’s interior state.
6. Preserve player-owned embodiment and consent as unresolved unless explicitly supplied.

## Completion

Stop when embodied detail expresses pressure without claiming universal meaning, changes the scene’s action or interpretation, and preserves viewpoint and agency limits.
