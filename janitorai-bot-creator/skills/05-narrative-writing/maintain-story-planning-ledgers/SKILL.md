# Maintain story planning ledgers

Track information that can create a continuity error or future story opportunity. Do not duplicate the manuscript or record facts with no downstream use.

**Action:** Record only consequential state, keep truth layers separate, and update the ledger when a scene changes knowledge, goals, relationships, or material conditions.
**Key point:** A ledger is a decision aid, not a second manuscript or a cage for improvisation.
**Why:** Small, dated state changes preserve continuity while leaving room for surprise and player-owned action.

## Define truth layers

Keep separate fields for:

- established story fact;
- character belief or interpretation;
- claim made in dialogue;
- author plan or possibility;
- player-controlled or unresolved information.

Never promote a belief, rumour, planned reveal, or discarded idea into canon without evidence in the narrative.

## Choose ledgers

Use only the tables the project needs:

### Character state

`time/scene | character | location | immediate goal | resources | condition | relationship shift | last seen | next pressure`

### Knowledge and disclosure

`fact/claim | truth status | knower | source | learned when | confidence | who they told | contradictions | reveal opportunity`

### Open threads

`thread | introduced | promise made | current pressure | responsible character | possible payoffs | deadline | status`

### Object and evidence

`item | owner/controller | location | known properties | believed properties | transfers | damage/use | continuity risk`

### Character change

`dimension | starting state | pressure/event | observable response | interpretation | later state | permanence uncertain?`

Add specialised columns only when they will be maintained.

## Update after meaningful changes

Record deltas rather than rewriting the whole profile. Update when a character learns, decides, promises, lies, loses access, changes allegiance, suffers a lasting consequence, relocates, reveals a capability, or leaves a thread unresolved.

Use stable identifiers for scenes, characters, and objects. Date or sequence entries in the story's chronology, not merely the order written.

## Preserve uncertainty

- Mark unknown, disputed, inferred, false, superseded, and author-only information explicitly.
- Record contradictory accounts side by side until the story resolves them.
- Track confidence when a character could reasonably be mistaken.
- Keep alternative future branches conditional; do not merge mutually exclusive outcomes.

## Use ledgers for drafting

Before a scene, retrieve only the participating characters, relevant location, active threads, accessible knowledge, carried objects, and recent state changes. Generate opportunities rather than mandates: a forgotten promise can create pressure without forcing a particular response.

Treat callbacks as opportunities, not commands. When a prior object, line, promise, wound, ritual, secret, or unresolved question becomes relevant, surface the smallest usable cue and let the current character decide whether to notice, remember, evade, reinterpret, or act. A callback is successful when it changes the present scene; it is not successful merely because it repeats a fact.

For each surfaced callback, record:

- the earlier evidence and who can access it now;
- the current trigger or reminder;
- the character's plausible readings and reasons to resist recall;
- the available actions, refusals, or disclosures;
- the consequence if the callback is used, missed, or misread.

Do not turn a remembered detail into an automatic emotional reaction. Keep player-owned memory, feeling, consent, and choice unresolved unless explicitly established.

After the scene, reconcile new facts and flag:

- impossible knowledge;
- location or possession conflicts;
- missing consequences;
- duplicated introductions;
- unexplained reversals;
- promises with no active pressure;
- changes asserted in notes but not evidenced in narrative.

## Protect roleplay agency

Treat player intentions, thoughts, feelings, consent, and future choices as unresolved unless the player has established them. Track player actions and explicit statements, not speculative interiority. Maintain separate branches when the interaction remains open.

Keep the ledger descriptive. It may record coercion, obsession, betrayal, injury, or other dark material without reframing it as mutual, therapeutic, or morally resolved. Do not use psychological labels as continuity facts unless the fiction explicitly establishes them and the user wants them tracked.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
