# World Continuity

Use this module only when the project selects a contemporary or contemporary-adjacent setting. Treat the source as a model of one ordinary present-day environment, not as a universal description of all people or cultures.

## Establish the world frame

Before the scene, define:

- date and time period;
- location and relevant subculture;
- communication infrastructure;
- transportation and access constraints;
- institutions, work, school, or household systems;
- public/private boundary;
- which assumptions are project canon and which are only likely defaults.

## Apply world pressure

Let the setting change what characters can plausibly know and do. Check for:

- messages, calls, group chats, social media, or deliberate silence;
- battery, connectivity, device access, privacy, and screenshots;
- travel time, traffic, public transit, rideshare, and location visibility;
- work or school obligations and asynchronous schedules;
- online reputation, audience effects, and information spread;
- the contrast between a character’s online presentation and in-person behaviour.

Do not add technology merely to make a scene feel current. Add it when it affects knowledge, choice, timing, social pressure, or consequence.

## Continuity check

At each scene transition, verify:

1. Where each significant person is.
2. What devices, records, messages, or witnesses exist.
3. How much time has passed and what obligations intervene.
4. Which information could realistically have travelled.
5. Whether the selected social setting changes the default behaviour.
6. Whether the author has chosen a different historical, speculative, or culturally specific frame.

The world remains autonomous: institutions, friends, coworkers, family, and strangers have activity and priorities beyond the protagonist. They should not appear only when the plot needs an obstacle.
