## Steps

1. Set source, period, place, community, class, language, genre, and intended creative use.
2. Separate identity, act, practice, public category, private conduct, medical claim, legal rule, character belief, and narrator framing.
3. Check anachronism, cultural flattening, outdated medicine, false prevalence, exoticisation, shock catalogue, and identity-act confusion.
4. Check unsafe practice copied from an encyclopaedia, therapist voice, narrator endorsement, and player-agency loss.
5. Compare variation by class, gender, generation, locality, status, faith, and personal history.
6. Verify current sexual-health, legal, or cultural claims instead of treating fiction or references as authority.
7. Preserve historical prejudice, fictional transgression, darkness, and ambiguity without presenting them as universal fact.
8. Keep {{user}}’s identity, desire, consent, speech, and action open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is a modern category projected backward unchanged? Label the comparison and add period terms. No: continue.
- Yes: Is a culture flattened into taboo or exotic practice? Add context, variation, and ordinary life. No: continue.
- Yes: Is unsafe practice presented as authority? Remove the instruction and verify claims. No: continue.
- Yes: Is the narrator endorsing or pathologising the material? Separate framing layers. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft decide {{user}}’s response? Remove it. No: continue.

## Output
