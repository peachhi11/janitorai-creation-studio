# Audit sexual culture and taboo framing

Find errors while preserving erotic charge, transgression, historical prejudice, secrecy, and morally difficult material.

**Action:** Identify source, period, audience, category, cultural variation,
current verification needs, and narrative framing.

**Key point:** A term's circulation does not prove universal meaning, prevalence,
safety, or timelessness.

**Why:** Provenance lets fiction retain taboo and history without turning a
source into a rulebook.

## Check the source frame

For every factual claim, ask:

- who produced the source, when, for whom, and for what purpose;
- whether it is research, history, memoir, advocacy, advice, dictionary, survey response, sensational compilation, or literary example;
- whether the claim describes one case, a recorded discourse, a community, or a population;
- whether current medical, legal, safety, identity, or demographic accuracy requires separate verification.

An encyclopaedia entry shows that a term or story circulated. It does not prove prevalence, universality, safety, or timeless meaning.

## Check cultural construction

Flag cultures portrayed as a single attitude. Restore variation by class, gender, age, religion, institution, locality, generation, and subculture. Separate official prohibition from private practice and selective enforcement.

Flag simple repression-to-liberation histories. Check who gained recognition, who remained excluded, and what new forms of surveillance or commerce appeared.

## Check words and categories

Flag modern identities projected backwards, dated clinical categories presented neutrally, and act/identity conflation. Preserve period language in character or document layers where appropriate, while keeping narrator and world truth distinct.

Do not assume explicit vocabulary proves confidence, silence proves ignorance, or a label fixes future behaviour.

## Check taboo and unusual-practice material

Flag shock catalogues, decontextualised anecdotes, anonymous self-reports treated as evidence, and dangerous or illegal practices copied as technique. Exclude sexual material involving minors from creative skill extraction. Do not convert bestiality, coercion, injury, or exploitation into neutral “variety” through list formatting.

Depiction is not endorsement, but the narrative must know what it is depicting. Preserve deliberate horror, abuse, prejudice, hypocrisy, or taboo without disguising its structure.

## Check medical and bodily claims

Flag dated anatomy, gender binaries, orientation theories, reproductive claims, contraception, STI, injury, diagnosis, and treatment advice. Route current health or safety claims to specialised verification.

## Check voice and sovereignty

Flag sociology lectures in dialogue, clinical-report character prose, moralising narration, and generic educational speeches. In interactive work, do not assign the player's identity, vocabulary, attraction, disgust, arousal, consent, history, speech, or action.

## Return the audit

For each issue, provide:

- exact claim or pattern;
- source, cultural, historical, medical, narrative, or agency problem;
- confidence and evidence gap;
- minimal repair;
- intended darkness, period texture, or transgression to preserve.

Do not replace one universal sexual morality with another.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
