# Audit intimate technique framing

Find errors without flattening explicit, awkward, selfish, dark, or morally difficult intimacy.

**Action:** Replace mechanical claims with viewpoint, selected sensation,
response, adjustment, meaning, and consequence.

**Key point:** Intimacy can be mismatched, selfish, coercive, awkward, or
excellent without becoming a performance score.

**Why:** Scene evidence is more useful than universal technique claims.

## Check mechanical writing

Flag step-by-step move lists, repetitive anatomy, named-technique catalogues, sensory laundry lists, and characters who perform perfectly without attending to a partner. Replace them with viewpoint, selected sensation, response, adjustment, meaning, and consequence.

## Check universal claims

Flag one correct sequence, gender formulas, fixed orgasm routes, guaranteed reactions, and gesture dictionaries. Restore individual preference, context, body variation, mismatch, and legitimate change.

## Check performance framing

Flag orgasm, speed, duration, swallowing, endurance, penetration, novelty, marks, erection, lubrication, or partner noise when presented as objective proof of skill, love, masculinity, femininity, surrender, or success. Preserve them as character beliefs when the fiction needs the resulting pressure or conflict.

## Check consent and escalation

Flag any assumption that a kiss permits touching elsewhere, that one accepted act permits the next, or that stillness, surprise, freezing, arousal, orgasm, or lack of resistance is consent. Require clarification when intensity, body area, restraint, pain, penetration, exposure, or risk changes materially.

## Check health claims

Remove or separately verify claims about STIs, contraception, infection, anatomy, injury, gag reflexes, oils, essential oils, numbing, food, temperature, breath restriction, pregnancy, medication, or medical conditions. This skill is not a sexual-health authority.

## Check voice, darkness, and sovereignty

Keep analysis author-facing unless technical language suits the character. Do not sanitise selfishness, manipulation, ignored feedback, incompatibility, humiliation, coercion, or deliberate violation. Name those dynamics accurately and preserve consequences. Do not assign the player's touch, arousal, body, pleasure, orgasm, consent, speech, action, or escalation.

## Return the audit

For each issue, provide:

- exact passage or pattern;
- framing or evidence error;
- narrative, bodily, or agency risk;
- minimal repair;
- intended darkness, awkwardness, or conflict to preserve.

Do not turn the audit into generic relationship advice or require healthy behaviour, therapy, disclosure, or technical perfection.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
