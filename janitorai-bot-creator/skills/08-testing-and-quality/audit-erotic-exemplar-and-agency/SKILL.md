# Audit erotic exemplars and agency

Separate narrative usefulness from behavioural endorsement.

**Action:** Identify the fictional promise, choice structure, narrative layers,
and agency risks.

**Key point:** Coercive or taboo depiction can remain in the story without the
audit rewriting it into a healthy relationship.

**Why:** Fictional genre logic preserves intended charge while keeping
real-world instruction, story causation, and player control separate.

## Check genre logic

Identify the promise made by the genre and tone: puzzle fairness, speculative
rules, symbolic or surreal causation, melodramatic consequence, or erotic
transgression. Preserve the promise without treating it as real-world advice.
An impossible event still needs an established rule, a deliberate rupture, or
a meaningful cost; otherwise the scene reads as arbitrary rather than charged.

## Classify the material

Identify whether the source is:

- a fictional scene or fantasy;
- a random encounter or game mechanic;
- a first-person account or claimed real event;
- a compulsion, hypnosis, transformation, or conditioning premise;
- an instructional or challenge format;
- a source using taboo, danger, or power as atmosphere.

Record what the form promises the reader or player and whether the text keeps that promise honestly.

## Audit the choice structure

Mark:

- who initiates, who knows, who can refuse, and who can leave;
- whether bypass, alteration, negotiation, and acceptance exist;
- whether a character’s silence, arousal, fear, intoxication, or prior relationship is treated as consent;
- whether the source distinguishes fantasy, roleplay, desire, action, and aftermath;
- what happens after a boundary is crossed, a tactic fails, or a secret is disclosed.

Flag railroaded erotic events, coerced “rewards,” magical or hypnotic guarantees, real-world medical or bodily-risk instructions, and language that makes predation sound inevitable. Preserve deliberate darkness by naming the danger rather than sanitising it.

## Audit narrative layers

Separate objective truth, character belief, narrator framing, reader inference, and player knowledge. A character may claim to be compelled or may eroticise coercion; the audit should not silently confirm either claim.

## Report

Return a table with: source element, craft value, unsupported claim, agency gap, safety or copyright risk, fictional framing required, and reusable transformation. End with what can be retained, what must be paraphrased or bounded, and what should be excluded from procedures.

Keep the author's darkness, kink logic, and unresolved consequences unless a
tonal change is requested. Do not infer the author's beliefs or the player's
desire from the material.
