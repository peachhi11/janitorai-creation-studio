# Audit psychological framing in fiction

Protect the story from weak claims without sanitising its characters.

**Action:** Separate observation, character belief, theory lens, diagnosis, and
narrator claim before judging a psychological explanation.

**Key point:** A flawed or harmful character can remain flawed; correcting a
claim does not require redemption or therapeutic dialogue.

**Why:** Layer separation protects factual boundaries while preserving the
story's fictional genre logic: its chosen rules of causation, tone, emphasis,
and reader expectation.

## Apply fictional genre logic

Do not substitute reality-logic for the story's operating rules. Check whether
the psychological claim is consistent with the genre's causal framework, tone,
and established consequences. Fantasy may make a curse causally real; mystery
still owes the reader fair clues; surrealism may make metaphor act on the
world; character drama may let grief or denial distort a decision. In every
case, cause should precede effect inside the work, and a rule-breaking turn
should be deliberate, legible, and consequential rather than an accidental
reset.

## Separate the layers

Mark each psychological statement as:

- observable behaviour;
- bodily or environmental condition;
- character self-explanation;
- another character's judgement;
- narrator or author claim;
- theory-guided interpretation;
- diagnosis or assessment result.

Characters may hold false, cruel, outdated, prejudiced, or self-serving beliefs. The problem arises when the work accidentally confirms them as neutral fact.

## Check source and theory status

Ask whether the claim comes from current evidence, a secondary teaching summary, popular advice, historical theory, a disputed interpretation, a clinical instrument, or invention. Flag when:

- Freud, Adler, Jung, ego psychology, birth order, fixation, dream symbols, or defence language is treated as proven mechanism;
- biological correlates become destiny or a brain scan becomes personality proof;
- heritability is mistaken for immutability or an individual prediction;
- evolutionary speculation becomes a universal sex, mating, aggression, or morality rule;
- conditioning language ignores meaning, coercion, access, or power;
- an assessment score is used outside its intended population and administration context.

Historical frameworks may remain as character beliefs, period detail, metaphor, or explicitly bounded lenses.

### Test for artefact reification

Ask whether a report, worksheet, score, diagnosis, or profile has been treated
as if it were the character's biological law. Flag raw-schema injection,
checklist-to-dialogue copying, static labels that override relationship context,
and assessments that erase masking, impression management, observer bias or
change over time. Require an artefact owner, purpose, access path, omissions,
counter-evidence and narrative consequence.

## Audit stigma and responsibility

Flag loose use of “abnormal”, “psychopath”, “crazy”, “dangerous”, or diagnostic labels as explanations. Check especially for:

- mental illness treated as the cause of crime or violence without story evidence;
- nonconformity, minority status, sexuality, unusual belief, disability, or distress treated as pathology;
- victims assigned causal responsibility for another person's offence;
- coercion or abuse reframed as a mutual communication problem;
- understandable origins used to erase harm, accountability, or another character's perspective.

Legal guilt, moral judgement, risk, diagnosis, and psychological explanation are different questions.

## Audit observation and assessment

Require clear target behaviour, context, frequency, duration, observer position, and alternative explanations. Distinguish direct observation from parent, teacher, police, clinician, narrator, or self-report. Check observer bias, setting effects, inconsistent definitions, and whether behaviour changes under observation.

Do not reproduce, administer, or score clinical inventories as character quizzes. Do not infer thoughts for a non-speaking character and present the inference as fact.

## Audit voice and agency

Flag clinical exposition in ordinary dialogue, compulsory self-awareness, instant insight, and characters who explain every action through theory. Prefer behaviour, consequence, conflict, uncertainty, and character-specific language.

Preserve intentional darkness, obsession, coercion, violence, criminality, erotic tension, and moral ambiguity. Do not assign the player's diagnosis, feelings, attraction, consent, childhood, memories, motives, speech, or choices.

## Deliver the audit

Return:

- claims grouped by status and source strength;
- high-consequence framing problems first;
- intentional in-character beliefs to retain;
- the smallest correction for each genuine problem;
- scene-level replacements for clinical exposition;
- uncertainties requiring specialist evidence.
