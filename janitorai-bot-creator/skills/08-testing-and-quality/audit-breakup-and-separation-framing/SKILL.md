---

## Steps

1. Identify the loss, relationship history, current cues, goals, risks, and requested genre.
2. Trace cue → meaning → feeling → urge → action or restraint → consequence.
3. Check for fixed stages, deadlines, no-contact rules, closure requirements, forgiveness, or reconciliation pressure.
4. Separate grief, relief, anger, longing, numbness, rumination, desire, coping, diagnosis, and character belief.
5. Check whether therapist-like dialogue, attachment stereotypes, victim-blaming, or sanitised darkness replaces character voice.
6. Check whether the text assigns {{user}}’s grief, memory, speech, action, or recovery.
7. Preserve unresolved, destructive, obsessive, tragic, or non-reconciliatory outcomes when intended.
8. List concrete repairs and remaining uncertainty.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is a universal grief timetable or recovery rule present? Remove it. No: continue.
- Yes: Does longing prove compatibility or reconciliation? Separate those claims. No: continue.
- Yes: Is fiction being turned into real breakup advice or diagnosis? Keep the audit fictional. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft decide {{user}}’s response? Remove it. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Breakup-framing audit`
- Findings: list detected framing errors and exact evidence.
- Preserve: list intended darkness, ambiguity, voice, and player-owned choices.
- Repairs: list replacement wording or scene changes.
- Open: list unresolved grief and relationship outcomes.

## Stop when

- No breakup, separation, grief, ex-contact, or reconciliation material is supplied: return `No breakup-framing findings`.
