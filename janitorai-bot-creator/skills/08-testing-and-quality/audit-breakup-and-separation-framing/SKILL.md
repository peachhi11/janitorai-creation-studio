# Audit breakup and separation framing

Find framing errors without sanitising the intended fiction or replacing it with generic relationship advice.

**Action:** Separate timing, viewpoint, contact, power, grief, and prescribed
outcomes before judging the aftermath.

**Key point:** Obsession, retaliation, ex-sex, reconciliation, or permanent
separation may be intentional narrative routes rather than errors to correct.

**Why:** A fictional-genre-logic audit restores specificity and player
sovereignty without moralising the premise. Check that separation follows the
work's tone, social rules, and established causal consequences rather than a
universal recovery script.

## Check causal overreach

Flag claims that:

- all initiators or recipients feel the same way;
- attachment style, gender, or one behaviour predicts a fixed breakup sequence;
- numbness proves trauma, healing, indifference, or lack of love;
- longing proves compatibility or destiny;
- intrusive thoughts reveal secret desire or diagnose OCD;
- one warm message, apology, or sexual encounter proves reconciliation.

Replace certainty with observable evidence, conditions, alternatives, and character-specific history.

## Check prescriptions

Flag compulsory no contact, closure, confession, friendship, forgiveness, therapy, reconciliation, dating, detachment, or permanent separation. Restore practical obligations, safety, culture, dependency, chosen values, and plausible alternatives.

## Check contact and power

Distinguish a boundary from punishment, silence from safety, persistence from coercion, consensual ex-sex from access assumed through history, and mutual reunion from pressure. Preserve stalking, manipulation, retaliation, obsession, and exploitation when intended, but name their function accurately in author analysis.

Also check what happens after physical separation. Continued access may run
through messages, shared accounts, location data, gifts, work, children,
mutual friends, finances, reputation, or “accidental” encounters. Do not treat
post-breakup contact as proof of mutual longing or as a neutral continuation of
the relationship. Map who can still reach whom, what refusal costs, what
evidence exists, and whether the contact changes safety, leverage, or narrative
choice.

## Check voice

Move polished diagnostic and therapeutic explanations out of ordinary dialogue unless the speaker plausibly uses them. Restore character-specific accusation, humour, bargaining, silence, ritual, evasion, contradiction, and action.

## Check interactive agency

Do not decide what the player misses, wants, forgives, remembers, consents to, says, or does. NPCs may pressure, misread, pursue, withdraw, or make claims; the narration must not convert those claims into the player's internal truth.

## Return the audit

For each issue, provide:

- the problematic claim or passage;
- the error type and likely effect;
- what evidence or distinction is missing;
- a minimal correction that preserves tone and darkness;
- any continuity or sovereignty risk that remains.

Do not turn an audit into treatment advice or demand morally healthy outcomes.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
