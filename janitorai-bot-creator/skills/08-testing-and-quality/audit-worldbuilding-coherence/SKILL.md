# Audit worldbuilding coherence

Find the failures that alter comprehension, choice, causality, character specificity, or trust. Do not reward a setting merely for containing more lore.

**Action:** Define the story promise, evidence, material life, timing, cultural
depth, consequences, and smallest repair.

**Key point:** Audit at the scale the story reaches; missing detail is not
automatically a defect.

**Why:** Focused checks improve usability without replacing imaginative freedom
with a worldbuilding checklist.

## Define the promise and evidence

Write three short statements:

1. what kind of experience the setting promises;
2. which characters and conflicts must use it;
3. what the current draft actually shows.

Separate established canon, disputed in-world belief, intentional mystery, draft placeholder, and accidental omission. Audit only at the scale the story reaches.

## Run the seven checks

### 1. Material life

Can people obtain food, water, shelter, energy, care, clothing, transport, tools, and waste removal? Who performs the necessary labour, who controls access, and who carries the danger or cost?

Do not assume every hierarchy is purely economic. Check law, force, ancestry, faith, citizenship, information, and status alongside material interests.

### 2. Why now

Identify the trigger that makes the story possible at this moment. Ask what previously prevented the event, what changed, who noticed, why actors cannot simply wait, and which restraints still operate.

If the answer is coincidence, decide whether it is an intentional inciting accident or an unsupported convenience.

### 3. Cultural depth and provenance

Check whether a fictional people is a lightly renamed real community or a bundle of borrowed visual markers. If so, research the source context and rebuild through history, institutions, relationships, internal variation, and individual subjectivity.

Similarity creates responsibility; changing names or adding fantasy anatomy does not remove it.

### 4. Internal variation

Test every nation, class, faith, species, institution, and political group for factions, regional differences, generational change, mixed interests, dissent, and uneven practice. Distinguish official history from memory, scholarship, propaganda, and rumour.

Variation should arise from position and experience, not random disagreement added to pass a checklist.

### 5. Historical contingency

Look for history that proceeds as if the strongest always win, institutions perform their intended function, and present borders or rulers were inevitable. Add only the contingencies the story needs: miscalculation, compromise, personality, weather, disease, succession, technology, logistics, accident, local resistance, or an unintended consequence.

Do not make history random for flavour. Preserve causal pressure, path dependence, and the persistence of older arrangements.

### 6. Lived place

Check whether scenes contain viewpoint-specific physical evidence: routes, effort, weather, sound, smell, texture, maintenance, work, wildlife, architecture, crowding, privacy, and familiar places. A detailed atlas cannot replace the feeling of moving through one street or room.

Remove scenic catalogues that do not affect action, relationship, understanding, or expectation.

### 7. Transformative consequences

For each major magic, technology, species, resource, historical divergence, or supernatural fact, trace:

- direct uses and costs;
- unequal access and gatekeeping;
- defensive measures and workarounds;
- effects on work, law, intimacy, war, art, crime, belief, and daily routine;
- second-order responses and unintended consequences;
- reasons older practices persist.

Do not require every imaginable consequence. Follow the branches that can touch the story.

## Check character and player contact

For every important setting fact, ask:

- who knows it and how;
- what they believe it means;
- what it lets them do or prevents;
- whether it changes a relationship or decision;
- whether the draft reveals it before it becomes necessary for a fair choice.

Do not use lore to dictate a player character's motives, belief, attraction, consent, feelings, or action. Do not treat intentional darkness, coercion, tragedy, prejudice, or moral ambiguity as a coherence flaw merely because it is uncomfortable.

## Prioritise the findings

Classify each issue:

- **blocking:** a choice, plot turn, or consequence cannot make sense;
- **distorting:** a group, culture, or character is flattened or misrepresented;
- **unstable:** rules, geography, knowledge, costs, or continuity change for convenience;
- **thin:** the setting lacks lived evidence or ordinary baseline;
- **unused:** detail consumes attention without affecting the work;
- **open by design:** uncertainty is intentional and its boundaries are clear.

Fix blocking, distorting, and unstable issues first. Leave open-by-design material alone.

## Make the smallest useful repair

For each priority issue:

1. state the contradiction or missing causal link;
2. choose the responsible setting layer;
3. add, remove, or revise the minimum fact needed;
4. trace its immediate ripple effects;
5. identify the scene, object, behaviour, rule, or consequence that will demonstrate the repair;
6. update the continuity record.

Route substantial repairs to the relevant focused skill: story-driven scope, world consequences, institutions, class and mobility, cultural context, geography, ecology, belief systems, or character-filtered disclosure.

## Report the audit

Return:

- a brief statement of what already works;
- findings ordered by severity, each with evidence and story impact;
- a concrete repair rather than a vague warning;
- dependencies and ripple effects;
- material that should remain ambiguous or undeveloped;
- a final check for character specificity, player sovereignty, and non-diagnostic framing.

Avoid scoring the setting with a single number. Coherence serves the requested story; it is not completeness, realism at any cost, moral approval, or conformity to one genre formula.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
