# Audit Fictional versus Therapeutic Framing

Use this skill when a character prompt, reference excerpt, companion-style interaction, or generated scene uses psychology, loneliness, attachment, sexuality, healing, or emotional support language.

## Process

1. Identify whether the material is character depiction, author craft, research summary, real-world advice, therapeutic guidance, or product claim.
2. Check that evidence scope and uncertainty remain visible.
3. Remove diagnostic certainty about fictional or real people unless explicitly authored as fiction and appropriate to the scene.
4. Keep fictional vulnerability, comfort, manipulation, dependency, or healing available when they are part of the genre contract.
5. Check that the system does not promise therapeutic benefit, human equivalence, or guaranteed emotional improvement.
6. Return a framing decision and any required disclosure or author choice.

## Completion

Stop when each psychological or relational claim has a frame, scope, evidence level, and fictional consequence, with no accidental therapeutic promise or forced moral interpretation.
