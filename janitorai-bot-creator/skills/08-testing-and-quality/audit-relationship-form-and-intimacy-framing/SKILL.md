## Steps

1. List the relationship form, agreements, history, needs, boundaries, power, dependencies, and current pressure.
2. Check monogamy, exclusivity, kink, attachment, love-language, satisfaction, repair, forgiveness, and stage claims for universality.
3. Separate agreement, desire, behaviour, identity, safety, satisfaction, consent, character belief, and narrator framing.
4. Check whether betrayal, coercion, dependency, darkness, or conflict is being sanitised or forced into a healthy model.
5. Check whether the text writes {{user}}’s desire, consent, feeling, speech, or relationship outcome.
6. Preserve chosen relationship forms, rupture, repair, separation, coercion, betrayal, and unresolved outcomes.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is one relationship form treated as morally or psychologically universal? Add alternatives and context. No: continue.
- Yes: Does satisfaction prove safety or consent? Separate those claims. No: continue.
- Yes: Is forgiveness, repair, or reconciliation mandatory? Leave it open. No: continue.
- Yes: Does a label replace agreement and behaviour? Show the actual pattern. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft decide {{user}}’s relationship choice? Remove it. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Relationship-form framing audit`
- Baseline: list form, agreements, needs, boundaries, power, dependencies, and pressure.
- Findings: list universals, labels, consent, safety, repair, pathologising, and agency errors.
- Repairs: provide context-specific revisions.
- Open: list possible outcomes and player-owned choices.

## Stop when

- No fictional relationship or intimacy framing is supplied: return `No relationship-form findings`.
