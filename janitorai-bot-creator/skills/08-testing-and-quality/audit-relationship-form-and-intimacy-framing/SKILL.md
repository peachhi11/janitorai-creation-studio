# Audit relationship-form and intimacy framing

Protect relationship diversity and agency without sanitising conflict or harm.

**Action:** Separate labels, agreements, practice, viewpoints, power, and
outcomes before judging the relationship's framing.

**Key point:** Relationship quality is not a single score, and a dark dynamic
does not need to be corrected into a safe one.

**Why:** Concrete distinctions preserve both agency and the author's intended
form, conflict, and ambiguity.

## Audit the form label

For every label—married, monogamous, open, polyamorous, casual, female-led, D/s, healthy, toxic, or otherwise—ask what concrete terms it denotes. Flag labels used as proof of consent, equality, fidelity, permanence, maturity, or abuse.

Distinguish public identity, explicit agreement, tacit expectation, private practice, and deliberate concealment.

## Audit consent and agreement

Flag communication treated as consent, a safeword treated as universal permission, previous agreement treated as permanent, or constrained acquiescence treated as free choice. Check alternatives, refusal costs, dependency, information, and current willingness.

Do not equate hierarchy with abuse or stated equality with safety. Require observable process.

## Audit intimacy and satisfaction claims

Flag sexual activity used as proof of emotional closeness, disclosure used as proof of trust, stability used as proof of quality, or satisfaction used as proof that no harm exists. Preserve multiple intimacy channels and each participant's separate satisfaction.

Check whether one partner's report has silently become the relationship's objective truth.

## Audit causal overreach

Flag inevitable outcomes assigned from Big Five traits, attachment labels, love styles, love languages, gender, trauma, kink, infidelity, or a fixed relationship-stage sequence. Replace destiny with conditional cognition, emotion, behaviour, stress, feedback, and alternatives.

Keep addiction, trauma, personality, and sexual diagnoses out of fiction craft unless established canon requires them.

## Test framework framing

For every popular or academic framework, ask whether it is being used as:

- an author-facing organising lens;
- a character's belief, defence, vocabulary, or weapon;
- a contested interpretation another character may reject;
- or an unsupported universal law.

Flag the last case. Love-language categories should not mechanically determine
compatibility; attachment labels should not automatically predict flight,
closeness, or breakup; seduction material should not guarantee compliance. The
dynamic version is usually stronger: a character can cling to a framework,
misapply it, use it to protect a self-image, or freeze when another person fails
to follow the expected script.

## Audit prescribed endings and voice

Do not require forgiveness, repair, disclosure, therapy, monogamy, openness, sex, marriage, reunion, or separation as the correct outcome. Preserve repetition, ambiguity, deterioration, pragmatic continuation, failed change, and tragedy.

Flag ordinary characters who speak in polished couples-therapy terminology. Use their own language of promises, needs, resentment, rules, betrayal, loyalty, fear, and desire.

## Audit player sovereignty

Do not assign the player's relationship form, attraction, arousal, intimacy, consent, satisfaction, loyalty, forgiveness, speech, action, or decision.

## Deliver the audit

Return:

- unsupported conclusions attached to labels;
- consent and agreement errors;
- collapsed intimacy or satisfaction viewpoints;
- deterministic trait, trauma, kink, or stage claims;
- prescribed endings and unnatural dialogue;
- intentional dark material to retain;
- the smallest revisions that restore specificity and agency.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
