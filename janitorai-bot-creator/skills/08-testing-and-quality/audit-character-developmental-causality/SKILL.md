# Audit character developmental causality

Check whether the character's history supports their present patterns without pretending that development is a formula.

**Action:** Trace several plausible pathways from experience to present behaviour, then test continuity, alternatives, and change conditions.
**Key point:** A backstory can inform a pattern without owning the whole character.
**Why:** Multi-causal, culturally situated explanations preserve specificity and contradiction.

## Audit each causal claim

For every claim that an earlier experience caused a later pattern, ask:

1. Was the event available and meaningful to the character at that age?
2. Was it isolated, repeated, or reinforced by later experiences?
3. What did the character do in response, and did it work then?
4. What other people, institutions, abilities, and cultural expectations shaped the result?
5. What protective relationships or contradictory experiences were present?
6. What keeps the pattern active now?
7. What evidence would make a different explanation plausible?

Rank claims as well-supported, plausible but partial, metaphorical, disputed, or unsupported.

## Detect deterministic shortcuts

Flag:

- one event used to explain an entire personality;
- a childhood stage treated as a permanent diagnosis;
- strict age milestones presented as universal;
- Freud-style fixation claims treated as established fact;
- ordinary habits equated with ADHD, personality disorder, or another diagnosis;
- every adult relationship described as a repetition of a caregiver bond;
- culture reduced to background decoration;
- suffering treated as the only source of depth;
- retrospective memory treated as complete historical evidence.

Preserve deliberate symbolism and period-appropriate beliefs, but label them as such in author notes.

## Test continuity and change

Check the pattern in ordinary life, intimacy, status conflict, danger, and a setting that resembles the past. Variation should follow stakes, roles, skills, cues, and current relationships.

For a growth arc, require a mechanism: new information, repeated safe or unsafe outcomes, practice, responsibility, changed power, a model, a community, a loss, or a decision followed by action. Insight alone need not erase a rehearsed habit. Relapse, partial transfer, and domain-specific change may be more convincing than total transformation.

When the backstory includes diagnosis, support, schooling, work, or an
intervention, audit the setting as well as the person. A new label may change
self-understanding, language, access, or social treatment without changing the
underlying history overnight. A programme may provide structure, mismatch,
agency, burden, or useful tools; it is not automatically cure, proof of
deficit, or a personality replacement.

## Protect character voice and agency

Do not repair causality by making the character deliver a clinical autobiography. Prefer behaviour, choices, consequences, remembered fragments, and conflicting accounts. Do not sanitise a dark or harmful character merely because their pattern has understandable origins; explanation is not excuse or compulsory redemption.

Do not infer or overwrite the player's childhood, memories, feelings, attraction, consent, motives, speech, or choices.

## Deliver the audit

Return:

- the developmental claims and their support level;
- deterministic or diagnostic shortcuts;
- missing mediators, reinforcers, and counterexamples;
- continuity and growth findings;
- the smallest revisions needed;
- scene tests for the repaired character.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
