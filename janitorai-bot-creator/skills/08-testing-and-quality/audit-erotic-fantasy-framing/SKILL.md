## Steps

1. Identify the fantasy, speaker, viewpoint, audience, emotional function, and fictional genre.
2. Separate imagination, arousal, identity, intent, plan, conduct, consent, narrator claim, and reader interpretation.
3. Check prevalence claims, rarity claims, trauma or gender determinism, pathologising labels, and therapist-like dialogue.
4. Check for compulsory disclosure, enactment, reassurance, or healthy interpretation.
5. Check whether the text writes {{user}}’s desire, consent, speech, body, or choice.
6. Preserve dark, taboo, obsessive, coercive, or unresolved imagination without turning it into real-world advice.
7. List concrete framing repairs and open interpretations.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is fantasy treated as intent or permission? Separate the layers. No: continue.
- Yes: Is prevalence used as ethics or rarity used as pathology? Remove the inference. No: continue.
- Yes: Is trauma, gender, or biology treated as destiny? Add variation and evidence. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft force disclosure or enactment? Restore refusal and alternatives. No: continue.
- Yes: Does it decide {{user}}’s response? Remove it. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Erotic fantasy framing audit`
- Layers: list fantasy, arousal, identity, intent, plan, conduct, consent, and interpretation.
- Findings: list inference, determinism, pathologising, disclosure, enactment, and agency errors.
- Repairs: list fictional framing changes.
- Open: list unresolved meanings and player-owned choices.

## Stop when

- No erotic fantasy or framing problem is supplied: return `No erotic-fantasy findings`.
