# Audit erotic fantasy framing

Correct unsupported inference without flattening explicit, taboo, dark, or morally difficult imagination.

**Action:** Separate fantasy content, function, real-world wish, consent, conduct,
and narrative framing.

**Key point:** A fantasy can remain morally difficult or unresolved without
becoming a plan, diagnosis, or permission.

**Why:** Category separation preserves erotic charge and player sovereignty.

## Check category errors

Flag any collapse among:

- fantasy content;
- fantasy function or sought experience;
- real-world wish;
- consent to a specific act;
- actual choice or conduct.

An imagined scenario does not grant permission, predict behaviour, or reveal a complete moral identity.

## Check prevalence claims

Require sample, date, culture, wording, and method before calling a theme common, unusual, or rare. Frequency does not determine consent, legality, safety, ethics, distress, or pathology. A common fantasy may depict an impermissible act; a rare consensual interest is not inherently disordered.

## Check causal overreach

Flag attempts to infer trauma, abuse, attachment, childhood experience, orientation, diagnosis, or gendered nature from fantasy. Correlation, psychoanalytic theory, anecdotes, and popular “core theme” models can generate hypotheses only. Preserve multiple explanations and uncertainty.

## Check obstacle and ambivalence logic

Flag “reluctance increases desire,” “forbidden means wanted,” or “arousal resolves refusal” when used to override agency. Longing, prohibition, power, anger, fear, guilt, shame, and ambivalence may intensify or inhibit arousal; none creates consent.

## Check voice, darkness, and scope

Keep analytic language in author notes unless it fits the character. Do not require disclosure, enactment, therapy, integration, healing, or healthy behaviour. Preserve taboo, obsession, hostile imagination, humiliation, corruption, coercive imagery, and contradiction when intended, while distinguishing depiction from permission.

## Check player sovereignty

Do not assign the player's fantasy, history, arousal, desire, memory, consent, interpretation, speech, action, limits, or willingness to enact anything.

## Return the audit

For each issue, provide:

- exact passage or pattern;
- unsupported inference or collapsed distinction;
- narrative and agency risk;
- minimal repair;
- intended darkness or ambiguity to preserve.

Do not moralise private imagination or treat discomfort as evidence that fictional material must be removed.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
