## Steps

1. Identify goals, power, access, dependency, information, vulnerability, boundaries, and exits.
2. Classify each act as persuasion, manipulation, pressure, threat, coercion, abuse, assault, resistance, or aftermath.
3. Trace attempt → interpretation → counterplay or compliance → result → responsibility and consequence.
4. Check for magical-control claims, objection defeat, manufactured dependency, covert seduction, gaslighting, and diagnosis by label.
5. Separate involuntary response, survival behaviour, silence, and consent.
6. Check whether the text writes {{user}}’s thoughts, consent, speech, body, or choice.
7. Preserve intentional predation, obsession, coercion, tragedy, and unresolved darkness with clear consequences.
8. Keep findings fictional and non-operational.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is coercion being called persuasion, seduction, or mutual misunderstanding? Name the power and harm. No: continue.
- Yes: Is the text teaching objection defeat or vulnerability targeting? Remove operational detail. No: continue.
- Yes: Is involuntary response treated as consent? Separate them. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft decide {{user}}’s agency? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Coercive influence and agency audit`
- Power: list goals, leverage, dependency, information, vulnerability, boundaries, and exits.
- Findings: classify acts, consent errors, agency loss, responsibility, and consequences.
- Repairs: list fictional, non-operational changes.
- Boundary: state what remains dark, unresolved, and player-owned.

## Stop when

- No fictional influence, coercion, manipulation, or power struggle is supplied: return `No coercive-influence findings`.
