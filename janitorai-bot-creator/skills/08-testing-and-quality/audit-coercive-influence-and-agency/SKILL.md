# Audit coercive influence and agency

Identify the actual structure of choice without moralising away dark fiction.

**Action:** Classify influence by options, information, penalties, dependency,
pattern, and responsibility.

**Key point:** Naming coercion does not require removing it from the fiction or
assigning the player's response.

**Why:** Concrete choice analysis distinguishes depiction, impact, and agency
without turning the audit into therapy or a moral lecture.

## Classify the influence

Use the narrowest supported category:

- **Influence:** attention, interpretation, preference, or action changes, intentionally or not.
- **Persuasion:** reasons or appeals leave meaningful room to evaluate and refuse.
- **Manipulation:** material intent is concealed, information is distorted, vulnerability is exploited, or apparent options are engineered.
- **Coercion:** threat, force, deprivation, dependency, or severe penalty makes refusal unsafe or unreasonably costly.
- **Abuse:** a pattern uses power and control to dominate, degrade, isolate, frighten, exploit, or injure.

Characters may use different labels. Audit the options, information, penalties, and pattern rather than accepting the speaker's description.

## Test meaningful refusal

Ask whether the person can:

- understand what is being requested;
- receive material information;
- say no without threat or disproportionate loss;
- leave or seek help;
- revise the decision;
- retain access to basic needs, relationships, reputation, and safety.

Agreement under constraint may still be a choice in narrative terms, but it is not automatically free consent.

## Flag unsupported control claims

Reject claims that NLP, embedded commands, a phrase, negging, scarcity, charisma, gaze, reward, withdrawal, or “subconscious triggers” can program love, attraction, obsession, sexual compliance, or loyalty. Reframe outcomes through the particular character's motives, history, interpretation, options, and subsequent decisions.

Do not reproduce operational scripts for concealed sexual influence, defeating objections, targeting vulnerabilities, isolating someone, or eroding self-trust.

## Audit patterned control

Look across time for interacting behaviours: intimidation, humiliation, isolation, surveillance, financial or practical dependency, threats, denial, blame shifting, reputation damage, intermittent tenderness, and violence. Do not require every element before recognising a controlling pattern.

When a scene claims someone has been psychologically “mindfucked,” audit the loop rather than accepting the label: trusted source or omission, false or distorted belief, emotional disruption, altered choice, and the evidence or counter-evidence that follows. Keep metaphor, philosophical analysis, and literal coercive control distinct.

Avoid remote diagnosis. Describe actions, option structures, consequences, and responsibility instead of declaring a personality disorder.

## Audit responsibility and voice

Flag narration that blames a target for trusting, freezing, appeasing, returning, desiring part of the relationship, failing to recognise a tactic, or lacking a safe exit. Understanding the influencer's wound or motive does not erase responsibility.

Keep analytical labels out of ordinary character speech unless they fit knowledge and setting. Preserve manipulation, coercion, abuse, predation, cruelty, obsession, violence, and moral ambiguity when intentional; do not force healing, exposure, redemption, or therapeutic framing.

## Protect player sovereignty

Do not assign the player's vulnerability, attraction, arousal, fear, consent, beliefs, memories, thoughts, speech, action, or submission. NPCs may pressure, deceive, threaten, or attempt control, but outputs must leave the player's response open.

## Deliver the audit

Return:

- influence classification with evidence;
- option and refusal-cost analysis;
- control pattern and escalation points;
- unsupported psychological or consent claims;
- target counterpower and missing alternatives;
- intentional darkness to retain;
- minimal corrections that restore agency without sanitising the scene.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
