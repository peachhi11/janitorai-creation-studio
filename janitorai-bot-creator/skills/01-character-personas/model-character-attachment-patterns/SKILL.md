# Model character attachment patterns

Treat attachment as a working pattern for managing valued connection, not a diagnosis or destiny. Separate what the character wants from what they expect, what they show from what they protect, and what they learned from what they choose now.

**Action:** Map desired closeness, feared consequence, cue, strategy, payoff,
cost, exception, and the other person's response.

**Key point:** Attachment language is a provisional lens; it does not decide
love, capacity, consent, or the ending.

**Why:** Context-sensitive patterns preserve both relational specificity and
character agency.

## Narrative intention and fictional genre logic

Attachment patterns serve the premise rather than a universal relationship
ideal. Preserve toxic, coercive, obsessive, erotic, tragic, avoidant, or
ambivalent dynamics when the story intends them; trace their rules and fallout
without forcing secure behaviour, reconciliation, or a healthy ending.

## Map the pattern

Record:

- desired closeness and feared consequence;
- working belief about self, other people, and dependence;
- cue that activates the pattern: silence, criticism, intimacy, uncertainty, conflict, separation, or care;
- first body response and attention shift;
- action strategy: approach, protest, test, appease, over-disclose, minimise, distract, withdraw, intellectualise, attack, or repair;
- short-term reward and long-term cost;
- what the character knows about the pattern, and what they misread;
- exceptions: safe people, different stakes, private versus public settings, exhaustion, or genuine incompatibility.

Use secure, anxious, avoidant, and fearful language as provisional shorthand only. A character can be confident in work, anxious in romance, avoidant during shame, and generous with chosen dependence. Do not infer a style from one cue.

Treat bonding as context-sensitive and bidirectional. Stress, pain, sleep,
trauma reminders, caregiving demands, cultural expectations, and the other
person’s responses can all alter the connection. Difficulty bonding in one
period or relationship does not prove absent love, fixed incapacity, or a
permanent attachment type.

## Keep the framework inside the character's lens

Attachment language may be an author's shorthand, a character's online theory,
or a contested label used during conflict. Do not let “avoidant”, “anxious”, or
“fearful” become a universal law that predicts every conversation. A character
may mislabel a healthy boundary as abandonment, use a label to excuse pursuit or
withdrawal, or discover that their pattern changes with a particular partner.
Let the label create interpretation, defence, shame, or leverage; let behaviour
and consequences decide whether it fits.

## Translate into scenes

Show attachment through initiation, response time, disclosure, requests, boundaries, conflict, touch, sex, repair, and aftermath. Let the same strategy produce different meanings: a delayed reply may be self-protection, punishment, overload, or ordinary busyness. Keep the character capable of choice, concealment, tenderness, and contradiction.

## Deliverable

Return an attachment map: need, fear, working belief, trigger, strategy, observable behaviour, interpretation by each person, payoff, cost, exception, and change pressure.
