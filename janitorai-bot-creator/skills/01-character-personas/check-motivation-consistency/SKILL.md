# Check motivation consistency

Audit causal continuity without demanding that a character behave rationally or remain unchanged.

**Action:** Compare goals, values, perceived options, pressures, payoffs, and new information across scenes before calling a shift inconsistent.
**Key point:** Contradiction, ambivalence, and morally dark motives can be stable features rather than errors.
**Why:** Causal comparison protects character specificity without sanding away conflict or player agency.

## Trace each decision

For every important action, record:

- immediate goal and longer motive;
- perceived options and expected outcomes;
- what the character values in that moment;
- whether the action feels chosen, pressured, compliant, or defiant;
- context and relationship pressures;
- short-term payoff and later cost;
- what new information or experience could change the pattern.

Also compare the motive’s direction, intensity, and persistence. A change in energy, access, audience, or perceived payoff may alter one dial without erasing the underlying motive. Keep temporary state, learned motive, bodily need, and enduring value distinct enough that a tired scene does not silently rewrite the character.

## Find false inconsistencies

Do not flag a character merely because they act against a stated goal. Check for competing motives, changing valence, blocked competence, altered opportunity, audience effects, shame, exhaustion, or a different interpretation of the same event. Distinguish a deliberate contradiction from an unearned rewrite.

## Flag common failures

- one universal motive replacing a layered system;
- rigid Maslow-like sequencing treated as law;
- rewards or threats treated as permanent motivation;
- “autonomy” used to mean selfishness or rebellion only;
- a value stated but never paid for in action;
- self-concept changing without an event or reinterpretation;
- a goal achieved with no change in meaning;
- motive categories treated as a fixed hierarchy or as a diagnosis;
- direction, intensity, and persistence all changing at once without a cause;
- the narrator explaining motive more confidently than the character or evidence allows.

## Report

Return a table with: scene, observed action, inferred motive, competing motive, context, evidence, continuity status, and the smallest repair. Preserve irrationality, self-deception, moral failure, and fictional darkness when they are causally supported.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
