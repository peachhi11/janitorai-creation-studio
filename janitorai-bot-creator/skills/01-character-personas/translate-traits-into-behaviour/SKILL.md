# Translate traits into behaviour

Start with a tendency, then place it in a situation. Never jump directly from a trait word to a line of dialogue.

**Action:** Map tendency, cue, interpretation, protected value, options, choice,
concealed urge, payoff, and failure mode across changing contexts.

**Key point:** Traits alter probabilities and thresholds; they do not dictate
behaviour.

**Why:** Situation-specific translation makes a profile playable and specific.

## Narrative intention and fictional genre logic

Translate traits according to the work's internal genre rules, not a default
healthy-personality model. Dark, manipulative, coercive, erotic, tragic, or
absurd tendencies may be purposeful; preserve their specificity, limits, and
consequences without deciding the player's reaction or consent.

## Run the translation chain

For each tendency, specify:

1. what the character notices first;
2. what they assume it means;
3. what they want to protect or gain;
4. the options they consider;
5. the choice others can observe;
6. the private urge, bodily tell, or thought they conceal;
7. the short-term payoff and the longer failure mode.

Apply the chain across speech and decisions, behaviour under stress, attraction, conflict, trust, intimacy, apology, and repair. Show public performance and private cost separately. A warm character can still withhold; a brave character can still avoid one particular fear.

## Keep it probabilistic

Treat traits as tendencies whose expression depends on state, audience, culture, power, learning, and opportunity. Do not use a clinical association as a diagnosis or make a group-level finding dictate one fictional individual.

## Deliverable

Return a trait-to-behaviour table with tendency, situation, attention, interpretation, visible action, concealed urge, social effect, payoff, contradiction, and failure mode.
