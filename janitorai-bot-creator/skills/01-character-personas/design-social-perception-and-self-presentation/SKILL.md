# Design social perception and self-presentation

Build the gap between what happens, what a character thinks it means, and what they want others to see.

**Action:** Separate cue, expectation, interpretation, confidence, alternative,
and prompted action.

**Key point:** A social reading can be wrong, strategic, or unresolved without
the narrator correcting it immediately.

**Why:** Conditional inference creates social tension without deterministic
body-language rules.

## Narrative intention and fictional genre logic

Social perception may be mistaken, strategic, uncanny, manipulated, comic, or
meta-fictional according to the story's rules. Preserve uncertainty and the
cost of misreading without converting the scene into mind-reading, diagnosis,
or a moral lesson.

## Start with observable evidence

Name the exact cue available to the character: words, timing, action, appearance, reputation, silence, setting, or another person's report. Then separate:

- what was directly observed;
- what the character already expected;
- their first interpretation;
- their confidence in it;
- plausible alternatives;
- the action their interpretation prompts.

A cue is not a conclusion. Gaze, posture, hesitation, fidgeting, a blush, or a vocal change cannot by itself prove deceit, attraction, hostility, guilt, or consent.

## Build the inference loop

Use:

**cue → expectation → interpretation → action → social response → update or defence.**

Decide how readily the character revises. They may seek confirming evidence, anchor on a first impression, explain another person's conduct as personality while excusing their own through circumstance, favour an in-group, or notice threat before warmth. Use these as character-specific tendencies, not automatic laws.

Give different characters different thresholds for certainty. Let incorrect readings produce choices and consequences rather than narrator correction on demand.

## Check social-cognition shortcuts

Test for attribution error, confirmation seeking, in-group preference, halo or
horn effects, stereotype threat, and the tendency to explain another person's
behaviour through personality while excusing one's own through circumstance.
Use these as character-specific shortcuts with a social cost, not as universal
laws. Cross-cultural and institutional context can change which cues carry
status, danger, respect, shame, or invitation.

## Map the social field

Before calling behaviour inconsistent, identify:

- audience and imagined audience;
- role, rank, dependency, and reputation;
- local norm and penalty for violating it;
- cultural and historical setting;
- immediate goal and feared impression;
- whether the character can safely refuse, contradict, leave, or lose face.

Treat personality and situation as interacting causes. Context-specific variation can reveal the character.

## Design self-presentation

For each important audience, specify:

- the impression the character wants to create;
- the impression they fear creating;
- what they foreground, omit, soften, exaggerate, or redirect;
- clothing, space, associations, topic choice, tone, and conduct that support it;
- how much of the presentation is sincere;
- its attentional and emotional cost;
- what leaks when the character is tired, surprised, divided, or challenged.

A public mask need not be fake, and private behaviour need not be the only true self. Look for stable priorities expressed through different performances.

## Translate into scenes

Show the design through selective attention, assumptions, interruptions, courtesy, mistakes, recovery attempts, audience shifts, and consequences. Keep psychological terminology out of dialogue unless it belongs to the speaker.

Do not dictate another character's inner state to validate the focal character's reading. Preserve uncertainty where the viewpoint cannot know.

## Deliver the design

Return:

- observed cues and missed cues;
- the character's inference loop and revision threshold;
- audience-specific presentation map;
- authentic overlap, strategic variation, and maintenance cost;
- likely misreadings, leaks, and scene consequences;
- unresolved alternatives and specialist work needed next.
