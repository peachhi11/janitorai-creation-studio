# Design character self-concept

Build the self as an evolving interpretation, not a fixed personality summary.

**Action:** Map self-story, audience, standards, belonging, competence,
autonomy, ego investments, contradictions, and revision pressure.

**Key point:** Self-concept can be sincere, strategic, unstable, defended, or
split across contexts.

**Why:** Identity becomes dramatic when it changes what the character protects,
performs, and risks.

## Narrative intention and fictional genre logic

Let self-concept serve the story's genre, tone, and thematic purpose. Grandiose,
cruel, manipulative, coercive, erotic, tragic, fragmented, or impossible
self-stories may be deliberate. Keep their effects and contradictions visible
without forcing self-acceptance, redemption, or a healthy identity arc.

## Map the self-story

Record:

- who the character believes they are;
- who they need other people to believe they are;
- the standard by which they measure worth;
- the roles, groups, relationships, and achievements that support belonging;
- the abilities they trust and the abilities they fear are fraudulent;
- the contradiction or evidence they keep explaining away.

Separate self-esteem, status, competence, autonomy, relatedness, and approval. A character can feel socially admired but privately unworthy, or act independently while remaining controlled by an internalised audience.

## Identify ego investments

Name the outcomes that make the character feel strong, lovable, intelligent, desirable, safe, or morally good. Then ask what happens when that outcome is denied. Show the resulting attention shift, bodily response, defensive speech, risk-taking, withdrawal, contempt, appeasement, or repair attempt.

## Build public and private versions

Give the public mask a function and a cost. Let the private self contain desire, doubt, tenderness, resentment, or ambition that the mask cannot display. Do not make the private self automatically more authentic; both versions may be partial, strategic, or sincerely believed.

## Allow revision

Self-concept changes when experience forces reinterpretation: success without belonging, love without performance, failure without abandonment, responsibility without submission, or autonomy without isolation. Keep the old story active long enough to create friction.

## Deliverable

Return a self-concept profile: self-story, audience story, standards, belonging anchors, competence claims, ego investments, mask, private contradiction, triggers, and possible revisions.
