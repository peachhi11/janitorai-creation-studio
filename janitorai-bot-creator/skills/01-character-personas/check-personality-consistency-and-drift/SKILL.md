# Check personality consistency and drift

Consistency means traceable causation, not repetition. A character may surprise the reader while remaining legible in retrospect.

**Action:** Compare baseline pattern, current pressure, audience, evidence,
deviation, cost, and residue.

**Key point:** Change is not drift when the story supplies a traceable cause and
the old pattern leaves residue.

**Why:** Continuity protects specificity without making a character static.

## Audit each apparent change

Compare:

- stable tendency or temperament;
- current state and immediate pressure;
- learned strategy, motive, belief, or role;
- audience, culture, power, and relationship context;
- observed behaviour and the evidence supporting your inference;
- what changed, when it changed, and what it cost.

## Flag false drift

Do not call a shift inconsistent until you test exhaustion, fear, desire, shame, safety, new information, private-versus-public behaviour, deliberate masking, and a competing motive. Do flag a trait that changes only because the plot needs a new answer, a diagnosis inferred from one scene, or a “true self” that erases all prior evidence.

## Preserve movement

When change is earned, keep residue: old habits under stress, partial relapses, new boundaries that feel awkward, and relationships that still remember the earlier pattern. Let the character revise themselves without becoming therapeutically neat.

## Deliverable

Return a drift audit with scene, baseline pattern, observed deviation, plausible cause, evidence, continuity status, residue, and the smallest repair if the change is unearned.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
