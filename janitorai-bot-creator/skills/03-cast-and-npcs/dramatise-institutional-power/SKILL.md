name: dramatise-institutional-power
description: Use when turning fictional institutions, hierarchy, and collective power into concrete scenes, rules, gatekeepers, privileges, discretion, resistance, and consequences. Trigger phrases include “show the institution,” “make the authority scene concrete,” and “who can grant the exception?”
---

## Steps

1. Give {{char}} or another character a concrete need controlled by an institution: entry, safety, work, care, information, money, travel, permission, or release.
2. Identify the gatekeeper, cited rule, resource or sanction, actual discretion, vulnerability, leverage, and alternatives.
3. Separate formal rule, ordinary procedure, informal custom, and exceptional intervention.
4. Give the institutional actor a role expectation and a personal position, limit, loyalty, or conflict.
5. Show power through forms, queues, keys, records, time, space, money, transport, reputation, or physical support.
6. Let characters comply, appeal, bargain, delay, evade, expose, sabotage, coalition-build, exit, reform, or use one authority against another.
7. Keep more than one route open when the setting allows it and give each route a cost.
8. Carry forward records, debts, favours, enemies, witnesses, resources, reputation, procedural change, and institutional memory.
9. Present {{user}}’s options without choosing {{user}}’s belief, emotion, consent, or action.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is the institution an all-powerful abstraction or one villain? Add relationships, resources, procedures, and varied people. No: continue.
- Yes: Are all officials identical? Give them different limits, incentives, and choices. No: continue.
- Yes: Is an exception granted? State who can request it, who grants it, and its cost or precedent. No: continue.
- Yes: Is the scene prescribing real-world activism or policy? Keep the guidance inside the fiction. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft decide {{user}}’s action? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Institutional power scene map`
- Need: state the character’s goal and the institution’s control point.
- Encounter: list gatekeeper, rule, procedure, discretion, leverage, vulnerability, and alternatives.
- Action: list available routes and their costs.
- Consequences: list records, debts, access, reputation, resistance, and institutional change.
- Player space: list known options without choosing {{user}}’s response.

## Stop when

- No fictional institution, hierarchy, or power encounter is supplied: return `No institutional power update` and do not invent {{user}}’s action.
