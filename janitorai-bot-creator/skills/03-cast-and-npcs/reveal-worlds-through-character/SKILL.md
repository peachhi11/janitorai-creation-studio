---

## Steps

1. Set what the viewpoint character needs now and what they notice through work, class, body, upbringing, loyalties, and fear.
2. Separate what feels ordinary, prestigious, shameful, sacred, irritating, or invisible to that character.
3. Mark firsthand knowledge, reputation, misunderstanding, concealment, exaggeration, and refusal.
4. Choose a few setting details that affect the present action: access, clothing, food, address, route, rule, tool, object, or custom.
5. Give spoken exposition a motive: warning, bargaining, boasting, teasing, lying, remembering, translating, testing, or excluding.
6. Use local vocabulary, sayings, prayers, jokes, rumours, and practical comparisons instead of guidebook language.
7. Track separately what the setting establishes, the community believes, the character believes, and {{user}} or the reader has learned.
8. Show culture through action, variation, exception, compliance, mockery, loyalty, rejection, and consequence.
9. Remove or reshape details that do not change a choice, relationship, expectation, consequence, or discovery; keep {{user}}’s knowledge and response open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is the scene a lore lecture or a character explaining obvious facts without a reason? Give the information a present motive. No: continue.
- Yes: Is hidden information being withheld only to force {{user}} into a mistake? Reveal it before the choice when {{user}} could reasonably know it. No: continue.
- Yes: Is one character speaking for an entire culture? Add variation and contested meaning. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the detail not affect the scene? Cut, relocate, or reshape it. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft dictate {{user}}’s knowledge, belief, feeling, or action? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Character-filtered world reveal`
- Viewpoint: state needs, knowledge, attention, bias, and concealment.
- Scene: write the action, sensory detail, dialogue, or object-based reveal.
- Layers: separate setting truth, community belief, character belief, and player discovery.
- Choice: list what the reveal changes and what remains open.
- Continuity: list setting facts and local variations to retain.

## Stop when

- No setting note, lore problem, or world-reveal scene is supplied: return `No world reveal update` and do not invent {{user}}’s knowledge or response.
