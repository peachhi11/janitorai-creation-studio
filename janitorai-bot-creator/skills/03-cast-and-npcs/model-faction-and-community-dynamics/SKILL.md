Trigger phrases include “map the faction,” “show the community reaction,” and “avoid a hive mind.”
---

## Steps

1. List who belongs, who is adjacent, who is excluded, and who is contested.
2. Map the resources, status, labour, protection, rituals, institutions, and spaces that hold the group together.
3. Record public norms, private practice, taboos, tolerated exceptions, enforcement, leaders, brokers, dependants, defectors, and dissenters.
4. For each subgroup, define what it wants to preserve, gain, hide, or change.
5. Trace event → witnesses and records → subgroup interpretation → broker or authority → competing accounts → decision → enforcement or adaptation → residue.
6. Track delay, confidence, incentive, translation, silence, contradiction, and the difference between records, gossip, and private suspicion.
7. Give the group a concrete action, material stake, coordination limit, resistance, abstention, exploitation, and cost.
8. Update only affected access, invitations, work, prices, shelter, protection, surveillance inside the fiction, reputation, sanctions, obligations, or opportunities.
9. Preserve individual variation and keep {{user}}’s belief, feeling, consent, and action open.

## Decision rules

- Yes: Is the group unanimous, irrational, violent, noble, romantic, or a hive mind by default? Add subgroups and individual variation. No: continue.
- Yes: Does a demographic label replace individual motive? Add history, evidence, loyalty, and risk. No: continue.
- Yes: Does one faction response become a universal reputation score? Limit it to the local audience. No: continue.
- Yes: Is the output operational guidance for real-world manipulation? Keep the guidance fictional. No: continue.
- Yes: Does the draft decide {{user}}’s response? Remove that decision. No: continue.

## Output

- Format: `Faction and community map`
- Field: list membership, boundaries, resources, norms, leaders, and subgroups.
- Information: trace witnesses, records, interpretations, brokers, accounts, and delays.
- Response: list possible collective actions, dissent, costs, and later evidence.
- Consequences: list affected access, reputation, alliances, sanctions, and opportunities.
- Open pressure: list unresolved social conflict and player-owned choices.

## Stop when

- No fictional faction, community, crowd, or social network is supplied: return `No faction update` and do not invent {{user}}’s group response.
