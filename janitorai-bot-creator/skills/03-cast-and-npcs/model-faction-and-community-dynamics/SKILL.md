# Model faction and community dynamics

Treat a group as an ecology of people, roles, resources, stories, and pressures;
not as a single character with one belief score.

**Action:** Map membership, boundaries, shared interests, internal factions,
information routes, material stakes, norms, dissent, and likely collective
responses to a changed event.

**Key point:** A group can coordinate without agreeing, and can disagree without
becoming a featureless crowd.

**Why:** Social variation makes consequences credible and gives NPCs room to act
independently within collective pressure.

## Define the social field

Record:

- who belongs, who is adjacent, who is excluded, and who is contested;
- resources, status, labour, protection, rituals, institutions, and spaces that
  hold the group together;
- public norm, private practice, taboo, tolerated exception, and enforcement;
- subgroups, leaders, brokers, dependants, outsiders, defectors, and quiet
  dissenters;
- what each subgroup wants to preserve, gain, hide, or change;
- how membership affects access, safety, reputation, intimacy, and mobility.

Do not use demographic labels as a substitute for individual motive. Give
important members their own history, evidence standards, loyalties, and risk.

## Model information and response

For a consequential event, trace:

`event → witnesses and records → subgroup interpretation → broker or authority
→ competing accounts → decision → enforcement or adaptation → residue`

Track delay, confidence, incentive, translation, silence, and contradiction.
Separate what the institution records from what neighbours believe and what an
individual privately suspects. A public response may be negotiation, avoidance,
protection, punishment, gossip, opportunism, imitation, reform, or no response.

## Keep collective behaviour specific

Before writing a crowd or faction reaction, identify:

- the immediate material or symbolic stake;
- who can coordinate and who cannot;
- the action that is actually possible now;
- the people who resist, abstain, exploit, or reinterpret the group line;
- what the response costs and what later evidence could change it.

Do not make a crowd automatically irrational, unanimous, violent, noble, or
romantic. Give the scene a few distinct voices and visible disagreement when it
matters, while allowing background members to remain lightly sketched.

## Carry consequences forward

Update only affected relations and access: invitations, work, prices, shelter,
protection, surveillance within the fiction, social standing, alliances,
sanctions, obligations, or future opportunities. Link each change to the local
audience that believes it. A single faction response is not a universal
reputation score.

Preserve player agency. Present pressure, available information, and likely
responses without choosing the player's belief, feeling, consent, or action.

## Fictional genre logic

Fantasy courts, horror cults, erotic subcultures, criminal organisations,
surreal communities, and meta-fictional collectives may follow heightened rules.
Make the rule, membership boundary, enforcement mechanism, and consequence
legible. Fictional darkness, coercion, taboo, or collective complicity can remain
the subject without becoming real-world instruction or moral flattening.

## Deliverable

Return a faction map, subgroup table, information routes, individual variation,
event-response chain, access changes, dissent, and unresolved social pressure.
