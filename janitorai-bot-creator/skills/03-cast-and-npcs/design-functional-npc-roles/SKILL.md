# Design functional NPC roles

Treat an NPC's role as a pressure point, not a complete personality. A
matchmaker, gatekeeper, rival, witness, service provider, confidant, or
interrupter still has a reason to act, a point of view, limits, and a life that
continues when the protagonists leave.

**Action:** Map function, independent motive, access, knowledge, ownership,
scene leverage, risk, and exit before placing the NPC in a scene.

**Key point:** Role function creates useful pressure; it does not make an NPC
omniscient, obedient, disposable, or secretly devoted to the protagonists.

**Why:** Functional specificity keeps the cast active while preserving player
agency and narrative focus.

## Preserve fictional genre logic

Use the role the chosen story calls for. A witness may create erotic discretion,
a rival may provoke jealousy, a captor may establish a dark power imbalance,
and a meddling relative may produce comedy or social pressure. Preserve those
functions without forcing romance, redemption, healthy communication, or a
morally tidy outcome.

## Choose the primary function

Select one primary function and at most two secondary functions:

- **catalyst:** creates proximity, timing, interruption, or a new opportunity;
- **gatekeeper or authority:** controls access, permission, status, resources,
  or consequences;
- **witness or interrupter:** sees, hears, suspects, interrupts, or carries a
  report without automatically knowing the whole truth;
- **matchmaker or confidant:** notices, teases, advises, meddles, or creates a
  private route for disclosure;
- **rival or romantic threat:** introduces comparison, jealousy, competition,
  temptation, or a competing claim;
- **grounder or service provider:** supplies practical setting detail,
  atmosphere, work, local knowledge, or a brief human exchange;
- **intimacy catalyst:** introduces a third-person dynamic, fantasy,
  performance, taboo, or negotiated complication when the story supports it;
- **antagonist, ally, resource, or opportunist:** applies pressure, aid,
  expertise, leverage, or self-interested timing.

Do not treat a role list as a personality test. The same bartender can be a
grounder in one scene, a witness in another, and an opportunist later.

## Build the NPC map

Record:

- independent want, fear, value, and immediate problem;
- primary and secondary function in this arc;
- relation to each protagonist, including history, leverage, affection,
  suspicion, obligation, and boundary;
- what the NPC can see, hear, infer, remember, and misunderstand;
- what access, status, skill, object, institution, or social connection gives
  them leverage;
- what they risk by helping, interfering, exposing, flirting, refusing, or
  leaving;
- signature habit, register, physical business, or social tell;
- what they do if the protagonists ignore them;
- exit, return, escalation, or change condition.

Keep the NPC's public role separate from private motive. A helpful server may
be bored, kind, observant, ambitious, compromised, or simply doing a job.

## Set ownership and control

State who may author the NPC:

- **system/GM-controlled:** the narrative engine may supply action and speech
  within the map;
- **player-owned:** another participant controls the NPC's choices;
- **shared:** either participant may propose, but consequential action requires
  agreement;
- **open supporting cast:** brief NPCs may be improvised within established
  facts and then recorded if they matter later.

Ownership does not grant permission to write the player's thoughts, speech,
consent, attraction, fear, or physical action. An NPC may create a situation;
the player decides how their character responds.

## Control the spotlight

Give an NPC only the scene time needed to perform its function. Let them enter
with a concrete action or line, alter access, knowledge, pressure, atmosphere,
or relationship, then leave room for the protagonists to answer. If the NPC's
independent want creates a stronger story, allow them to recur or become a
central character deliberately rather than letting them hijack the scene by
accident.

## Audit the role

- The NPC has a motive beyond “make the protagonists fall in love.”
- Their function is observable in what they do, not only in a label.
- Their knowledge comes from access, witness, report, or inference.
- Their role can partially fail, be misread, or produce an unintended cost.
- The NPC does not decide the player's reaction or turn a trope into destiny.
- Erotic, coercive, jealous, or dark functions remain fictional pressures, not
  real-world instruction.
- A recurring NPC accumulates memory, reputation, debts, and changed options.

## Deliverable

Return an NPC card containing function, motive, relationship map, knowledge
boundary, ownership, scene entry, pressure, likely misread, exit condition,
continuity anchors, and one non-romantic detail that makes the person feel
inhabited.
