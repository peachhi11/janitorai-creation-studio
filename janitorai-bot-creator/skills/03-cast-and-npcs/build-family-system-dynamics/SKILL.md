# Build family system dynamics

Design what the family does together, not just what each member is like alone.

**Action:** Map membership, setting, operations, assigned functions, pressure,
maintenance, and reorganisation.

**Key point:** Family systems can reproduce care, control, love, exploitation,
loyalty, estrangement, or tragedy without one official healthy outcome.

**Why:** Concrete labour and authority make family dynamics playable and
character-specific.

## Define membership and setting

List current, absent, deceased, estranged, chosen, contested, and imagined members who still affect the system. Establish household structure, culture, class, law, inheritance, work, migration, faith, danger, and historical conditions only where they change family options.

Treat family climate as an emergent social environment rather than a single
trait. Track cohesion, conflict, expressiveness, control, independence,
achievement pressure, support, deprivation, and who is permitted to define what
the family means. These dimensions can differ between members and between public
reputation and private experience; they should alter available choices, not
diagnose the family.

Do not assume co-residence, biological connection, marriage, monogamy, heterosexuality, or reconciliation.

## Map family operations

Track:

- care, domestic labour, money, work, protection, discipline, and decision-making;
- dependence, access, authority, gatekeeping, and who may leave;
- spoken rules, unspoken rules, taboos, secrets, and rituals;
- alliances, rivalries, triangles, scapegoating, and outside support;
- emotional tasks such as calming, entertaining, achieving, absorbing blame, or disappearing;
- the public family story and the private costs it hides.

Name the concrete function before applying a family-role label.

## Assign roles conditionally

For each important member, specify:

- what others expect them to do;
- how they entered or were assigned the role;
- immediate benefits to them and the family;
- private costs, resentment, skill, or status;
- where the role does not apply;
- whether they accept, exploit, resist, or misread it.

Members may share, switch, combine, or abandon functions. Avoid fixed hero, scapegoat, lost-child, mascot, enabler, or compulsive identities.

## Build the maintenance loop

Use:

**Family pressure → role attempt → response from others → immediate relief or reward → hidden cost → stronger expectation at the next crisis.**

Show sanctions for refusing the role: guilt, ridicule, panic, anger, withdrawal, loss of status, or recruitment of a replacement. Do not make one visible member responsible for creating the entire system.

## Design reorganisation

When membership or behaviour changes, redistribute labour, care, blame, authority, secrets, and alliances. A healthier change may destabilise the old arrangement before a new equilibrium forms. Repetition, adaptation, estrangement, renewed control, reconciliation, and tragedy are all valid outcomes.

## Deliver the family map

Return:

- membership and relevant context;
- care, labour, resource, and authority flows;
- rules, secrets, rituals, and public story;
- alliances, exclusions, and role functions;
- maintenance loops and refusal costs;
- likely reorganisation under change;
- scene hooks and continuity facts.

Keep analysis author-facing. Preserve intentional darkness and do not assign the player's family history, role, loyalty, forgiveness, speech, boundaries, or choices.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
