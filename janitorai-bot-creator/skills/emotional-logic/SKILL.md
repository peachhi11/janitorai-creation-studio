# Emotional Logic

Use this module when a character is becoming generic, inconsistent, or overly dependent on prohibitions such as “never lies” or labels such as “mysterious.”

## Replace rules with causes

Rewrite a rule as a behavioural loop:

```yaml
trigger: what happened or was noticed
interpretation: what the character thinks it means
felt_pressure: emotion, bodily response, or competing need
motive: what they want to protect, obtain, avoid, or conceal
choice: the action they select now
cost: what the choice creates or leaves unresolved
```

Example pattern:

```yaml
trigger: someone asks for a direct confession
interpretation: answering plainly would surrender control
felt_pressure: warmth mixed with panic
motive: remain connected without becoming exposed
choice: answer partially, then ask a question in return
cost: the other person receives evidence of care but not certainty
```

This is a tendency, not a mechanical prohibition. A character may break the pattern when the pressure, knowledge, values, or stakes change.

## Replace labels with signals

Translate adjectives into repeatable, variable evidence:

- “mysterious” becomes selective disclosure, evasive answers, controlled pauses, or a history of protecting information;
- “confident” becomes what the character volunteers, refuses, risks, and assumes they can repair;
- “kind” becomes who receives attention, what discomfort they tolerate, and where they draw limits;
- “jealous” becomes the character’s interpretation, monitoring behaviour, rationalisation, and response to uncertainty.

Give each trait at least one behaviour when safe, one under pressure, and one contradiction. Avoid making one cue prove an entire personality.

## Preserve subtext

Dialogue should carry both the literal exchange and the active pressure beneath it. Before writing a line, identify:

1. What the character wants the other person to understand.
2. What they refuse to say directly.
3. What they fear the other person will infer.
4. What changes if the other person responds well or badly.

Use voice—vocabulary, rhythm, humour, silence, formality, and emotional shifts—to show the character’s strategy without narrating a diagnosis.
