# Audit character cognitive continuity

Check the chain from event to present claim. Preserve intentional unreliability while finding accidental omniscience and convenient forgetting.

**Action:** Trace availability, perception, attention, encoding, retention,
retrieval, inference, claim, and disclosure permission.

**Key point:** Unreliability can be intentional, strategic, or partial; a gap
needs a mechanism rather than a plot convenience.

**Why:** Cognitive continuity protects mystery and viewpoint without forcing
certainty.

## Build the continuity chain

For each consequential fact, mark:

1. **Available:** was it physically or socially accessible?
2. **Perceived:** did the character's senses register it?
3. **Attended:** did it receive enough priority amid competing demands?
4. **Encoded:** was a usable trace formed?
5. **Retained or changed:** what happened across delay, interference, retelling, and new information?
6. **Retrieved:** what does the present cue make accessible?
7. **Inferred:** what has the character worked out beyond direct memory?
8. **Claimed:** what do they choose to say, imply, conceal, or lie about?

Do not collapse these states. A character may know without remembering the source, remember without disclosing, infer correctly from incomplete evidence, or lie about a true memory.

## Audit attention before memory

When a detail is missing later, first ask whether it was noticed. Check viewpoint, distance, lighting, noise, language, distraction, divided tasks, expertise, emotional priority, and scene timing. “It was in the room” does not establish that every character encoded it.

When a detail is recalled, require a plausible route: direct attention, later testimony, documentary evidence, repeated rehearsal, established expertise, or a cue that reconnects the trace.

## Classify discrepancies

Label each mismatch as one of:

- supported difference in perspective;
- plausible omission or reconstruction;
- deliberate concealment or deception;
- new inference from available evidence;
- authorised change after learning something new;
- unresolved ambiguity;
- accidental continuity error;
- impossible perception or knowledge leak.

Do not “fix” an authored lie, biased recollection, or dark motive merely because it is inaccurate. Fix the causal support or presentation when the text accidentally treats it as neutral truth.

## Check behavioural consistency without freezing the character

Compare the present scene with the character's established tendencies for noticing, task-switching, recall detail, confidence, and recovery from error. Allow change when the story supplies practice, fatigue, intoxication, injury, fear, altered stakes, a new relationship, or deliberate strategy.

Consistency means intelligible variation, not identical performance in every scene.

## Protect roleplay boundaries

Keep world truth, character memory, character inference, character claim, and player knowledge separate. Do not repair a gap by inventing a player's prior words, feelings, attraction, consent, memories, motives, or actions. A player's assertion is not automatically canon, and a non-player character's confidence is not narrator confirmation.

Keep corrections in ordinary narrative language. Characters should not recite audit states, cognitive models, or therapy-style explanations unless their role genuinely calls for it.

## Deliver the audit

Return:

- a fact-by-fact continuity chain;
- supported ambiguities and intentional distortions to retain;
- errors ranked by narrative consequence;
- the smallest repair for each real error;
- regression tests for later scenes or roleplay turns.

Prefer local repairs: change a sightline, add a cue, move a revelation, weaken certainty, establish prior attention, or mark a line as inference. Do not rewrite the character's whole psychology to solve one continuity fault.

## Narrative Intention and fictional genre logic

Treat the skill as a tool inside a chosen fictional genre, not a universal moral or psychological law. Dark, erotic, coercive, obsessive, tragic, comic, surreal, or unresolved material may be intentional. Preserve causal consequences, character specificity, truth-layer boundaries, and player sovereignty; do not turn a fictional pattern into diagnosis, a healthy-relationship lesson, or a real-world operational manual.
