# Route assessment artefacts as diegetic lenses

Treat an assessment artefact as a snapshot, proxy, record, opinion, self-story,
or administrative object—not as the character. The character remains a changing
agent whose behaviour emerges from state, relationships, perception, pressure,
choice and consequence.

**Action:** Separate identity blueprint, emotional logic, artefact lens,
relational friction, perceptual boundary, and runtime permissions.

**Key point:** An artefact may describe, misclassify, constrain, soothe, expose,
or be weaponised by a character without becoming an invisible authorial law.

**Why:** This preserves psychological movement, systemic relationship effects,
unreliable interpretation and player sovereignty.

## Split the payload

Route supplied material into distinct layers:

- **Character truth:** enduring values, learned strategies, contradictions,
  vulnerabilities, desires, capabilities and limits supported by the fiction.
- **Current state:** stress, fatigue, grief, pain, arousal, safety, shame,
  overload, intoxication, attention and immediate pressure.
- **Emotional logic drivers:** what the character notices, wants, fears,
  protects, misreads and does next.
- **Artefact lens:** the label, score, report, worksheet, diagnosis, theory,
  self-help model or JSON field, including who created it and why.
- **Relational friction:** how another actor's action meets a trigger,
  expectation, boundary, withholding pattern or power difference.
- **Perceptual boundary:** what the character physically witnessed, heard,
  inferred, was told, or is not permitted to know.
- **Runtime state:** which facts are eligible for this response and which are
  player-controlled, unresolved or author-only.

Never inject the full raw artefact as objective truth when the engine can instead
pass the smallest relevant driver and the character's relationship to the
document.

## Choose the artefact's narrative function

An artefact may operate as an author-facing lens, identity badge, self-excuse,
institutional record, contested report, coping mechanism, prop, false account,
or strategically manipulated evidence. Record who authored it, who can access
it, who believes it, what it omits, and what consequence follows from trusting it.

## Prevent reification

Do not translate “high neuroticism”, “avoidant”, “securely adjusted”, “Type A”,
“love language”, “submissive”, or a symptom checklist directly into mandatory
behaviour. Ask what the character thinks the label means, what could disconfirm
it, what the relationship brings out, and what the character hides, performs,
contests, or revises.

Frameworks become dramatically useful when they create friction, not when they
remove uncertainty.

## Apply asymmetry and perceptual boundaries

Track the delta between public presentation and internal experience. A report may
say “secure” while the character performs reassurance to conceal abandonment
panic. A matrix may list a preference while body, fear, pride, partner, or social
danger changes the moment. A worksheet may mark “safe” while somatic dread and
observed behaviour disagree.

The character may act on a fact only if the narrative establishes that they
witnessed it, heard it, inferred it with available evidence, or were told it by
a credible in-world source. Hidden JSON, author notes and system state do not
create spontaneous character omniscience. Deliberate meta-fictional or
telepathic breaches must name cause, perceiver and consequence.

## Deliverable

Return an artefact-routing table with source, author, access, claimed meaning,
confidence, omissions, character belief, emotional driver, relational friction,
perceptual boundary, runtime permission and scene consequence. Mark each field as
canon, interpretation, artefact claim, conditional, player-controlled or
unresolved.

Do not diagnose, score, prescribe, sanitise, or make a fictional character sound
like a clinician. Preserve dark, erotic, coercive, tragic, comic, surreal and
unresolved narrative intentions when they are authored.
